The Matchwork Method

Everything we install has to match something

A roof meets brick. Siding meets trim. Gutters meet both. Mortar meets brick that has been weathering since the house was built.

Free estimate Shingle matching and match tests

Why the company is called Matchwork

A color chart is not a match

A swatch is two inches square, printed on coated paper, read under showroom lighting. Your house is a few thousand square feet of material that has baked through Ohio summers and frozen through February nights. Those two things do not compare.

So the sample comes to the house. Full-size shingle boards, full-length siding courses, gutter sections, brick units and mortar pats, held against what is already there and read in the light the house actually sits in. We come back at a different hour and read them again, because the same shingle can look brown at nine and gray at four. The decision is made where you stand — the drive, the walk, the patio — not on a ladder.

  1. Read what is already there

    Brick, trim, roof, gutters, stone, mortar color, and how much of it has weathered. Photographed in the light we will choose in.

  2. Bring full-size samples to the elevation

    Not a chip. Enough material to see the blend across a whole course, with two or three options side by side.

  3. Read them twice, then order

    Morning and late afternoon, sun and overcast. Nothing is ordered until you have seen it on your own wall.

Roofing

Shingle to brick

Brick is not one color. It is a body color, a fleck, a firing variation from unit to unit, and a mortar color that may be a third of what you see from the street. Read at the same time: trim, the gutter under the eave, stone on the lower elevation, a porch roof in the same sightline. A shingle chosen against the brick alone is half a decision.

Three things get compared, never one. Color: the blend in the shingle against the body and fleck in the brick, read from the drive. Shadow blend: how the laminated layers throw a shadow line, and whether it reads as depth or as noise. Granule texture: the size and density of the granule surface, which is what makes a new slope look new next to a weathered one, and what a sample board shows tilted into the sun.

This applies across roofing in Columbus and architectural and Class 4 shingles. Where the question is a match test rather than a color choice, see shingle matching.

Siding

Siding to trim

Color is the easy half of siding. Geometry and finish are what give a partial repair away.

  • Profile — the shape of the lap, the bead at the bottom edge, the reveal of a board and batten. Same color, different profile, is not one wall.
  • Exposure — how much of each course shows. A different exposure reads as a band across the elevation even when the color is exact.
  • Sheen — a finish chalked and faded for a decade next to a new panel off the pallet. Sheen is what makes the new panel look wet.
  • Color lot — the production batch. The same label in a different lot can shift enough to see, so a partial repair is checked on the wall before it is ordered.
  • Discontinued profiles — siding no longer made, where the work is finding the closest surviving profile or replacing a whole elevation.

The trade is siding in Columbus, hard cases on discontinued siding matching, trim on soffit, fascia and trim repair.

Gutters

Gutter to palette

A gutter is a continuous line around the house at the height the eye travels, in a finish that is either part of the palette or a stripe across it.

  • The roof — shingle color and the shadow line under the eave, which the gutter sits against most of the year.
  • The trim — fascia, rake boards, window surrounds. Matching trim hides the gutter; matching the roof makes it deliberate.
  • The brick — a white run against red brick is the most visible exterior decision on a masonry elevation.
  • The downspout — especially the one that runs past a window or down a corner. It is a vertical line in the middle of an elevation and gets the same attention as the run.

Copper and half-round are a different conversation: that metal is chosen to be seen, and it changes color as it weathers. The trade is gutters in Columbus, with seamless aluminum for the color-matched run and copper and half-round gutters where the house calls for it.

Masonry

Brick and mortar matching

Masonry is the hardest match of the four: the wall has been changing since the day it was laid, and the material you would match it with may no longer exist.

Brick that is no longer made. A Columbus house built decades ago was laid with brick from a yard that may have closed, in a size and color range no catalog carries. We compare size, body color, fleck, face texture and how the unit was fired, and we look at salvage before new. When nothing matches, the options are a deliberate contrast, a panel replaced, or a veneer.

Mortar color. Mortar is cement, lime, sand and pigment, and the sand does most of the coloring. A joint mixed with the wrong sand reads as a gray grid over the wall however close the pigment was. We mix a pat, let it set, and compare it dry, because wet mortar is always darker.

The color of age. A new unit is clean and sharp; a wall is not. Matching what the wall looks like today, and tooling the joint the way the original was tooled, is the difference between a repair you point out and one you look for.

The trade is masonry in Columbus, with brick matching and repair and tuckpointing.

The honest limits

What matching cannot do

Weathering

New material has not been through a decade of ultraviolet light on a south-facing slope or a hundred freeze-thaw winters. It reads newer, and on some surfaces it stays newer for a while. We never promise that a patch on a weathered surface will be invisible.

Reformulated blends

Granule blends, pigments and profiles change over the years, sometimes without the name on the label changing. The color you remember ordering is not always the color that arrives, so the sample is checked at the house before an order goes on the wall.

Discontinued lines

Some siding, brick and shingle lines are simply gone. You get the options in plain language — closest surviving match, one elevation replaced, or a deliberate contrast — before anything is ordered.

Matching is a craft judgment made at your house with a sample in hand. It is not a laboratory result, and nothing here promises an outcome we have not seen the materials for. Repairs that depend on a match are scoped on exterior repairs in Columbus; matching on a transaction runs through the realtor repair program.
