Seamless gutters

5-inch and 6-inch seamless aluminum

Seamless means the run is formed to your eave length at your house, so the only joints left are the ones that have to be there: corners and outlets.

Sizing

Five-inch or six-inch is a roof question, not a preference

Three things decide it, in this order.

  • Roof area draining to the run. A simple gable over a ranch feeds one run. A hip roof with dormers breaks the same eave into several shorter ones.
  • Pitch. A steep slope sheds water faster, so the same rain arrives harder and in a shorter window.
  • Valley concentration. Two valleys dumping into one outlet is the common mistake. The gutter may be sized right and the outlet still cannot pass the water.

Five-inch K-style is enough for most single-run eaves at a moderate pitch. Six-inch goes in where the roof area is large, the pitch is steep, or a valley collects mid-run. Going bigger than the roof needs buys nothing; going smaller because the truck only carried five-inch coil is how a clean, well-pitched gutter still overspills in a July downpour.

We measure the roof before we quote the gutter, the same discipline we bring to shingles and siding. That is written up on the Matchwork method page, and the full range sits on the gutter installation hub.

Outlet sizing follows the same logic. A correctly sized run drained by a small outlet still backs up, so outlet and downspout are specified with the gutter rather than after it.

On-site forming

The machine comes to your driveway

A forming machine on the truck takes flat painted aluminum coil and produces a continuous gutter at the exact length of your eave.

That matters twice over. Length: the run is one piece end to end, so there is no field seam mid-eave where most leaks start. Fit: it is cut to the measurement taken on your fascia, not to a stock increment that has to be joined and trimmed. Corners are mitered and sealed, outlets are punched and set, and the run is hung the same day it is made.

Coil comes in a wide range of factory colors, and where none of them lands against your trim we match to a sample. That decision happens at the house, against fascia and brick.

Hanging it

Hangers, spacing and the bracket argument

A gutter full of water and ice is heavy. Everything depends on what the hanger bites into.

Screw-in hangers

A long screw through the gutter face into fascia and the sub-fascia behind it. The strongest option on solid wood, and our default where there is real dimensional lumber behind the board.

Hidden bracket systems

A bracket the gutter snaps into, with no fastener showing through the face. Cleaner line, better on some profiles. It only holds if the bracket lands in solid wood.

Spacing and pitch

Consistent spacing, tightened at corners and either side of an outlet where load concentrates. The run is pitched to the outlet, not hung level, and checked before it is filled with water.

Worth naming: spikes-and-ferrules on old gutters. A long nail through the face into fascia that has gone soft holds for a while, then works loose each winter and the run sags between fasteners. If yours is spiking, the fascia is the thing to look at.

Diagnosis

What a seam failure actually looks like

A jointed gutter is sealed at every connection, and sealant is the shortest-lived part of the assembly. It hardens, shrinks, and loses adhesion where metal flexes in the sun. Then it opens.

From the ground you see it three ways: a dark vertical streak down the fascia below the joint, a pale mineral deposit on brick or a concrete stain under the eave, and a strip of washed-out soil or dead grass at the drip line where the ground takes the same hit every rain.

Up close the joint is discolored, the sealant has pulled away from one lip, and there is often a black algae line inside the gutter at the low side. In winter the wet joint freezes first and opens further.

Re-sealing one joint is a legitimate repair and we do it when the rest of the run is sound. When several joints on the same eave have gone, the run is finished and forming one continuous piece is the better answer. Localized fixes are handled through the gutter section of our exterior repairs page.
