Guides

Roofing and exterior guides for central Ohio

Plain explanations of what we actually see on houses around Dublin, Powell, Westerville and Columbus — written so you can look at your own roof, siding, gutters or chimney and know what you are looking at, whether or not you ever call us.

Nothing here is a sales pitch and none of it is a diagnosis of your house from a distance. What these guides do is name the failure mode, explain the mechanism behind it, and tell you what is ordinary wear and what is worth a closer look. Freeze-thaw through February, ice dams on a low pitch, wind-driven rain off Hoover Reservoir, straight-line wind in July and hard UV on a south-facing slope are the four things that age an exterior here, and most of what we write about comes back to one of them.

Roof diagnostics

The guides, in the order to read them

What granule loss in your gutters is actually telling you

Why a shingle is covered in granules at all, the normal shedding on a roof that is still new, what real granule loss looks like further along, and why one slope goes bald years before the others do.

Why a small nail pop can cause a real leak

How decking movement works a fastener back out, why that turns into a slow leak that stains a ceiling somewhere else, how to spot one from the ground or the attic, and what a repair that holds involves.

Hail bruising versus blistering, and how to tell them apart

The physical checks that separate an impact fracture in the mat from a manufacturing blister, how a test square is run on each slope, and why the difference matters before anything is filed.

If one of these sounds like your house

Send a few photographs and tell us which slope or which elevation they came from. We will say plainly whether what you are seeing is ordinary wear, a repair, or nothing at all yet. A roof inspection is the way to get that answer from on top of the house rather than from the ground, and the repairs page sorts the smaller jobs by trade.
