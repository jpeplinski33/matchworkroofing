Matchwork Roofing — logo concepts, sheet B

Roofline / shingle / “match” direction. No letters in any mark. Palette is spec §4 only: navy #1a2a5e, amber #f5a524, pale gold #f8c65c, white. Files: logo/b/<concept>/mark.svg, lockup-light.svg, lockup-dark.svg. Marks are paths only; wordmarks use live text (Sora 700 → Inter → Arial).

01

Ridge

logo/b/ridge/ — two shingle colours meeting at the ridge, inside a navy tile

One gable split down the ridge — left slope amber #f5a524, right slope pale gold #f8c65c, navy hairline between them as the ridge — sitting on a white wall with a deep eave overhang, so the two shingle tones are the whole idea and the house underneath is what makes it a roof rather than a peak. The navy rounded tile borrows the confidence of the live “MR” chip the owner already likes, stops the silhouette from ever reading as an upload arrow, and is already an app icon and a favicon without being redrawn.

02

Running bond

logo/b/courses/ — five staggered shingle courses that build a gable

Five courses of shingle tabs in a staggered running bond, each course overhanging the one above so both rakes come out serrated the way a real shingled edge does — the stagger is the craft signal, and the sawtooth keeps the diagonals from ever resolving into a clean arrow. The third course is amber against navy above and below it: that is the matched course dropped into an existing roof, and it is the one detail that survives as a band long after the individual keyways have closed up.

03

Half slope

logo/b/halfslope/ — a house with one slope replaced in amber

A gable house drawn as a thick roof band over a plain wall, with the walls stopping exactly at the roof’s inner edge so the eave overhang is a real 10-unit projection on each side — and the right slope filled amber, one plane re-roofed and the other left as found, which is the view a homeowner is describing when they call about a match. It is the quietest of the three and the fastest to register as “house” in a crowded search result, at the cost of being the closest to a generic home glyph.
