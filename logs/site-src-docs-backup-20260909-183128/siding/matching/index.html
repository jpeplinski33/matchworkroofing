Siding matching

Matching siding that is no longer made

Four damaged panels on a wall whose profile left production fifteen years ago. This page is for that problem, and it promises no miracle.

Free estimate How matching works at Matchwork

Why siding stops matching

Four things change between your wall and the shelf

Siding is a manufactured product with a lifecycle, and Columbus houses outlive plenty of those lifecycles. An owner with a damaged section is shopping for one specific siding, from one era, that nobody makes any more.

  • Profile. The shape of the lap and how panels interlock. Profiles get redesigned, and a new one will not lock into the old course even when it looks close in a photo.
  • Exposure width. How much of each panel shows. Half an inch shifts every course line, so a patch at the wrong exposure throws the elevation out of rhythm.
  • Colour lot. Vinyl colour runs through the panel and varies between production runs; fiber cement colour is a factory finish that gets reformulated. Two panels in one nominal colour, years apart, are not the same colour.
  • Weathering. Fifteen years of UV and freeze-thaw have changed your wall. Even genuine new-old stock from the same lot reads as a patch beside it.

The honest split

What can be matched, and what cannot

Usually matchable

Current-production fiber cement in a standard lap and a common colour. Engineered wood panels, where profile and exposure are set on site. Painted wood trim and cedar shakes, cut, sealed and finished to a sample. Vinyl soffit and aluminium trim wrap, where coil runs still exist. And almost anything on a wall that gets painted afterwards.

Often not matchable

Vinyl in a colour and profile out of production, where the panel will not interlock with the course above. Fiber cement in a reformulated colour. Anything with a moulded woodgrain texture since retooled. And any wall where a matched panel sits beside twenty years of fading, because the match then fails on age, not product.

We tell you which of the two you are in before you spend anything. A contractor promising an invisible patch on a discontinued vinyl wall is selling a surprise.

When nothing matches

Three options that are better than a bad patch

Salvage from the house

Take panels from an elevation nobody sees — behind a dormer, inside a porch, at a rear gable — and use them on the visible repair. Same product, lot and weathering. The donor area gets a substitute nobody looks at.

A planned panel swap

Move the mismatch somewhere it reads as deliberate. Replace a defined field — a gable, a garage bay, a dormer face — in the new material with a trim break at each edge, so the change looks designed, not damaged.

Replace one elevation

Sometimes the honest answer is a full wall. One elevation in a new material with its own trim lines reads better than three patches, and it deals with whatever is failing behind the old siding.

How the match happens

Identification before ordering

  1. Photograph and measure the elevation

    Exposure, panel width, thickness, profile, texture and the colour as it exists in daylight, not on a phone.

  2. Pull a board

    A removed panel shows the manufacturer's markings, the back profile and the true colour where sun never reached.

  3. Search stock, then salvage

    Suppliers first, then leftover stock and reclaimed material. If neither turns up the profile, we stop and present the options.

  4. Sample it on your wall

    The candidate goes up against the existing field, on your elevation, at the hour the sun hits it.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Before a closing

Discontinued siding is a common inspection remedy

An inspector writes “damaged siding, patch repair noted” on a house whose profile left production a decade ago, and a buyer, seller and agent need an answer inside a contingency window. The answer is usually one of the three options above, in writing, with photographs.

That is what the Close-Ready realtor program is for: the scope, dated photographs and the completion letter for the buyer's file. The repair menu across all four trades is siding repairs in Columbus; the material picture behind a one-elevation replacement is the siding hub.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about siding matching

Can you paint a patch to blend it in?

On wood and fiber cement, yes, within the limits of a weathered field. On vinyl it is a compromise: adhesion suffers, and the colour will not move with the panel as the original does.

Will a photo tell you what I need?

A photo gets us close and saves a trip. Exposure, thickness and the back profile still have to be measured from a real panel, so we pull one before ordering.

What if the whole wall is failing, not one section?

Then matching is the wrong question and replacement is the right one. We look at the substrate and price the elevation properly, in fiber cement, engineered wood or vinyl.
