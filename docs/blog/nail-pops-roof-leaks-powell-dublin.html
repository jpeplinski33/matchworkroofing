Nail Pops Explained: Why Roofing Nails Back Out & Cause Hidden Leaks

A "nail pop" is one of the most common roof defects in Central Ohio and easy to miss from the ground. A fastener that backs out can push through the shingle above it and let water reach the roof deck.

What Is a Nail Pop?

A nail pop happens when a roofing nail works loose from the decking and lifts the shingle above it. Once the shingle is pierced or lifted, rain can follow the nail into the roof deck.

1. Common Causes of Nail Pops

  • Thermal Expansion & Contraction: Ohio's hot summers and freezing winters cause roof decking to expand and contract, which can slowly work smooth-shank nails loose.
  • Improper Nailing: When nail gun pressure is set wrong, nails end up overdriven (cutting into the shingle) or underdriven (left proud and crooked), and neither holds the way it should.
  • Poor Attic Ventilation: Excess attic moisture can soften the underside of the decking and weaken its grip on the nail.
