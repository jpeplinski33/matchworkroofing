What Shingle Granule Loss Actually Looks Like on Aging Central Ohio Roofs

By Matchwork Roofing & Exteriors · September 5, 2026

If you find colored grit at the bottom of your downspouts after a heavy rain in Upper Arlington or Dublin, your shingles are shedding granules. Here is how to tell routine wear from a roof that is nearing the end of its life.

The Short Version:

Granules are not just for color. They shield the asphalt beneath from sunlight. Once enough granules are gone to expose dark asphalt or the fiberglass mat, the shingle ages quickly.

1. Why Asphalt Shingles Have Granules in the First Place

An asphalt shingle has three main layers: a fiberglass mat, an asphalt coating, and a surface of ceramic-coated mineral granules. The granules do three jobs:

  • Sun Protection: Bare asphalt breaks down in direct sunlight. Granules shade it.
  • Fire Resistance: Granules are part of the shingle surface. Fire ratings apply to the roofing product and assembly as a whole, rather than to the granules alone.
  • Weather Protection: Granules protect the surface from ordinary weathering. Storm impacts can still damage the shingle.

2. Normal Gutter Sediment vs. Critical Granule Loss

Not all granule shedding indicates a failing roof. Here is how Central Ohio homeowners can tell the difference:

Normal Weathering

  • • Light granule runoff early in a roof's life, when loose surface granules from manufacturing wash off.
  • • Shingles maintain uniform color and texture across all roof slopes.
  • • No shiny fiberglass threads or dark asphalt spots visible from the ground.

Signs of Advanced Wear

  • • Deep black "bald spots" visible where granules have completely dislodged.
  • • Shimmering, shiny silver fiberglass strands exposed to sunlight.
  • • Gutters that repeatedly fill with granule sediment.
  • • Shingle edges beginning to curl, cup, or crack.

3. What to Do If Your Roof Shows Granule Loss

Bald spots or exposed fiberglass deserve a closer inspection. The extent of wear and the condition of surrounding shingles help determine whether a repair or replacement makes sense.
