How to Spot Hail Damage on Your Roof: A Central Ohio Homeowner's Guide

Hail rarely punches a hole you can see from the driveway. On asphalt shingles it leaves bruises — and the easiest way to read a roof after a storm is often everything around the shingles. Here's how to size up your home after hail moves through Central Ohio.

The Short Version:

Start at ground level. Dented gutters, downspouts, and soft-metal vents tell you hail hit your home and roughly how hard. Shingle bruises confirm it up close. Photograph what you can see, note the storm date, and get a documented inspection before the evidence weathers away.

1. What Hail Actually Does to a Shingle

A hailstone striking an asphalt shingle knocks granules loose at the point of impact and can bruise or fracture the fiberglass mat underneath. From the ground a bruise can look like a dark spot; up close it feels soft or spongy compared to the surrounding shingle. A bruised mat can't hold granules the way it should, and that spot ages faster than the rest of the roof.

Two details separate hail hits from ordinary wear:

  • Pattern. Hail hits cluster on the slopes that faced the storm, with a random scatter within those slopes. Wear from age shows up more evenly across the whole roof.
  • Freshness. Recent hits expose darker asphalt where granules just came loose. Older bruises weather over and blend in.

One common point of confusion: heat blisters can look like hail hits from a distance but come from the shingle itself, not a storm. We break down the difference in Hail Impact vs. Blistering: How to Tell Them Apart.

2. Where to Look From the Ground First

You don't need a ladder for the first pass. Soft metals dent more easily than shingles bruise, so they act like a hail gauge for your own house:

Around the Roofline

  • • Gutters and downspouts: dings and dents along the face
  • • Ridge vents and metal flashing: visible dimpling
  • • Roof vents and pipe boots: dents on metal caps

Around the Yard

  • • AC condenser fins: bent or flattened spots
  • • Mailbox, grill cover, patio furniture: fresh dents
  • • Siding and window screens: chips or tears on the storm-facing side

If the soft metals are dented, the roof took the same storm — that's the point where a close-up roof inspection is worth doing.

3. Central Ohio's Hail Season

In the Columbus area, hail rides along with spring and summer thunderstorms — most of it falls between March and August, with the strongest cells typically in late spring. Storms are hyper-local: one Dublin street can take quarter-size hail while the next neighborhood over gets rain. After any storm with reported hail, it's worth a five-minute walk around your house even if your roof looks fine from the street.

4. What to Do After a Hail Storm

  • Note the date. The storm date anchors everything that comes after — inspections, documentation, and any conversation with your insurance company. Policies set their own deadlines for reporting damage, so ask your insurer what yours allows.
  • Photograph what you can reach. Dented gutters, downspouts, AC fins, and anything else at ground level. Date-stamped photos from your phone are fine.
  • Stay off the roof. Bruised shingles are fragile, slopes are slick after a storm, and a fall is a worse outcome than any leak. The roof-level check is our job.
  • Get the roof inspected and documented. A proper inspection photographs each elevation, marks test squares on the storm-facing slopes, and records the soft-metal collateral damage alongside the shingle condition.

5. If the Damage Is Real, the Next Question Is the Repair

Localized hail damage sometimes means a repair; widespread bruising across multiple slopes usually points toward replacement. Either way, the shingle on your roof may be discontinued, and weathering changes color over time — so whether new shingles will match the old ones is its own question. When it matters, we document your shingle and can arrange independent lab identification through our shingle matching (ITEL) process.

Replacing the roof is also the moment to consider a more hail-resistant shingle. Class 4 impact-rated options like CertainTeed NorthGate® ClimateFlex® are built for exactly this cycle — we compare them on our Landmark shingle page.
