What Gets Compared

Four Reasons a Repair Shingle May Not Match

Close is not a match. Ohio's matching rule names three things a repair has to match: quality, color and size. These are the ways a repair shingle falls short:

Dimensional Mismatch

Shingles have been made in different sizes and exposures over the years. A new shingle of a different size does not lay in cleanly with the existing courses.

Granule Color Fade

Granules weather and shift in color with years of sun exposure. A factory-fresh shingle placed next to weathered ones usually stands out.

Product Discontinuation

Manufacturers retire product lines over time. When yours is discontinued, the lab report says so in writing and names the closest shingle still made. That is the shingle we test on your roof.

Brittleness / Repairability

Older shingles can become brittle. Lifting them to slide in a replacement can crack the surrounding shingles, so a small repair can cause additional damage.

How It Works

Shingle Match Testing: What to Expect

1

Inspection & Photos

We inspect the roof and photograph the damage and the existing shingles, from the roof and from the yard.

2

Read the Repair Offer

We read your carrier's estimate line by line to see exactly what repair is being offered: which slopes and how many shingles.

3

Shingle Identification

We arrange for a sample of your shingle to go to an independent laboratory such as ITEL, which identifies the product, reports whether it is still made, and names the closest shingle still available. The lab is independent. The errand is ours.

4

Match Test and Your Carrier

We lay the closest available shingle on your roof next to the originals and photograph it. The lab report, the photos and Ohio's matching rule then go to your carrier with our line-by-line estimate.

The Result

What You Receive

Product Identification

The laboratory identifies the manufacturer and product of your shingle. It does not decide whether a mark is hail. That question is answered on the roof, in the inspection photos.

Production Status

The report states whether the product is still in production. It turns “we think it is discontinued” into “here is the report.”

Match-Test Photos

Photos of the closest available shingle laid on your roof next to the originals, taken from the roof and from the yard, the way anyone looking at your home would see it.

The Report Is the Middle of the Work, Not the End

You are not handed a lab report and wished good luck. MATCHWORK™ puts the lab report, the match-test photos and a line-by-line estimate in front of your carrier, together with Ohio's matching rule, which applies when your policy pays replacement cost:

“When an interior or exterior loss requires replacement of an item and the replaced item does not match the quality, color or size of the item suffering the loss, the insurer shall replace as much of the item as to result in a reasonably comparable appearance.” (Ohio Adm. Code 3901-1-54(I)(1)(b))

We meet your adjuster on the roof to show the mismatch in person, and you get a copy of everything we send. A repair that cannot be matched is a scope question, not a shrug. See the whole storm claim process, start to finish.
