What It Looks Like

Stone-Roof Presence, Asphalt Practicality

Belmont's color palette runs to deep blacks and grays and rich browns — shades like Black Granite, Colonial Slate, Shenandoah, Stonegate Gray, and Weathered Wood — laid over a thick, dimensional tab that throws real shadows. The effect from the curb is a quarried-stone roof, the kind historically reserved for homes with the structure to carry actual slate.

Because it's asphalt, a Belmont roof installs without the structural reinforcement, specialty labor, or maintenance a true slate roof demands — and unlike real slate, individual damaged pieces are straightforward to replace.

Who It Suits

Steep Roofs, Long-Term Owners

Belmont belongs on steeply pitched, highly visible rooflines — the kind you see on larger Colonials and Tudors in Upper Arlington and Bexley, and on custom builds in New Albany and Dublin where the roof can be a third of the home's visible exterior. It's aimed at owners who plan to stay and want the roof to be a permanent design feature.

It's also the practical pick among CertainTeed's slate looks for hail-prone Central Ohio: the Class 4 impact rating is the highest the industry tests to. If you prefer a scalloped, old-world silhouette over Belmont's straight-edged slate dimension, compare it with Carriage House below.

Manufacturer Warranty

Coverage From CertainTeed

CertainTeed backs Belmont with a lifetime limited transferable warranty against manufacturing defects on residential applications, 10-year SureStart protection, a 30-year StreakFighter algae-resistance warranty, and a 15-year 110 mph wind-resistant warranty — with an upgrade to 130 mph available when the roof is installed with CertainTeed starter and CertainTeed hip & ridge accessories.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a Class 4 impact rating actually mean?

UL 2218 is the industry impact test for roofing: steel balls are dropped onto the shingle from set heights, and Class 4 is the highest classification — the shingle withstands the largest impact without cracking. In Central Ohio hail seasons that rating is meaningful, and some insurers offer premium discounts for Class 4 roofs. Ask your insurance agent what your specific policy offers.

How is Belmont different from Carriage House?

Both are CertainTeed slate-look shingles with a UL 2218 Class 4 impact rating, and both carry the same manufacturer warranty stack. The difference is the silhouette: Belmont has straight-edged, deeply dimensional tabs that mimic quarried slate blocks, while Carriage House has a scalloped, hand-crafted cut with old-world character. We bring samples of both so you can see which suits your home's architecture.

Who handles the manufacturer coverage on my shingles?

Manufacturer warranties are provided and administered by CertainTeed — ask us which manufacturer's warranty applies to the shingles on your estimate, and review the full terms on CertainTeed's warranty page.
