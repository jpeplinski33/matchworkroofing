Board and batten

Board and batten in Hardie, LP and vinyl

One profile, three substrates, and a spacing decision that changes how tall your house looks from the street.

Free estimate How we match siding to a house

The profile

Boards, battens, and a shadow that does the work

Board and batten is a vertical wall: wide boards edge to edge, with a narrow strip — the batten — fastened over each joint. It started on barns because it was fast to build and shed water down the face, and came back as the vertical accent on gables, entries and farmhouse fronts.

What people like about it is the shadow line. Every batten throws a narrow vertical shadow that moves through the day, so the wall reads as planes instead of one flat color. That is why cheap versions look wrong: a shallow molded shadow does not survive midday sun.

Spacing

Batten spacing is a proportion decision

  • Wide spacing on a small gable reads heavy and short; tight spacing on a long wall reads busy.
  • Spacing follows the board width, which follows the stud layout, because the batten has to land on something solid at the joint.
  • Corners set the rhythm: the first board at an outside corner decides whether two adjacent walls read as one wall.
  • Openings interrupt the run. We lay the wall out around windows and doors first, so no batten lands awkwardly at a casing.

A vertical wall shows every deviation. Anything out of plumb at a corner reads down the whole elevation, so layout takes longer than fastening.

Three substrates

Which material holds the line

Fiber cement

Cement board strips with HardieTrim battens give the deepest, crispest shadow and the most durable face. Heavy, slower to cut, every cut end sealed. It still looks sharp a decade on a sun-hit Columbus wall. Detail is on the James Hardie page.

Engineered wood

SmartSide carries vertical panel and board-and-batten profiles and is the easiest of the three to keep straight on a tall gable: lighter to handle, faster to cut around openings, stable enough that a long run does not wave. Cut ends sealed, finish coat maintained. See LP SmartSide.

Vinyl

Vinyl board and batten is one molded panel, so the shadow is shallower and the joints are the panel's own. Lightest and lowest cost band, and it can look right on a simple gable or a rear elevation. On a long tall front wall it shows every unevenness behind it. See insulated vinyl.

Trim and transitions

Corners, windows and the meeting line

Outside corners take a corner post sized for a vertical wall, not a lap-siding corner. Inside corners need a channel that lets the board move without opening a visible gap.

Window and door surrounds carry head flashing above every opening, and the casing sits proud of the batten face so water sheds off the trim, not behind it. Where a vertical wall meets a horizontal one — a water table, a brick first floor, a lap-sided lower story — the transition trim and the flashing behind it decide whether the junction stays dry.

Soffit and fascia are part of the same finish pass; a vertical wall stopping short of a rotted fascia just moves the problem up. Soffit, fascia and trim repair covers it.

Proportions

What vertical lines do to a facade

Vertical boards pull the eye up, so board and batten adds apparent height. On a two-story colonial front or a tall gabled dormer that is a good trade. On a long low ranch it fights the roofline, which is why many ranch houses wear it on the front gable only, with lap siding below.

The two mistakes we fix are putting it everywhere and putting it nowhere near a trim break. A vertical accent works when bounded: one gable, one entry bay, one side wall, with a real corner and transition at each end. A darker body with a lighter batten exaggerates the shadow; a single color flattens it.

That judgment is what the Matchwork method is about: choosing profile and color against the house you have, brick, roof and roofline included.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about board and batten

Can it go over the siding I already have?

Almost never. A vertical wall needs a flat, plumb, solid nailing base, and lap siding is none of those. We strip to the sheathing, check it, wrap it and add strapping where framing calls for it.

Does a vertical wall leak more in wind-driven rain?

Not when it is detailed correctly. Water runs down the face faster than on lap siding, but every joint is a vertical seam, so the wrap, opening flashing and batten coverage all have to be right.

Is board and batten more expensive than lap siding?

Usually: more layout, more pieces, more trim. How much more depends on the substrate and the openings that interrupt the run. We measure your elevation and put the band in writing.
