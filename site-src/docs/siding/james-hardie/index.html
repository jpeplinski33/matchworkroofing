Why fiber cement here

A Columbus wall asks a lot of a siding board

Fiber cement is sand, cement, cellulose fiber and water, rolled into a board that does not rot, does not warp through a wet week, and does not feed carpenter ants or termites. It takes freeze-thaw without splitting, does not dent when a ladder leans on it, and holds paint far longer than bare wood.

It is also heavy and brittle in handling, which is why the details matter. A board fastened wrong cracks at the fastener. A board butted tight against its neighbor buckles in July heat. A cut edge left unsealed wicks water into the substrate. None of that shows on the day the job is finished.

This page is the installation story; the color and profile reasoning is on the Matchwork method.

To spec

Six details we do not shortcut

All of them invisible when the job is done. All of them visible a few seasons later if they were skipped.

Weather-resistive barrier and flashing

The wrap is lapped shingle-style, so anything behind the siding drains down and out. Flashing goes at every window head and sill, every penetration and every roof-to-wall transition, with kickout flashing where a roof ends against the wall.

Fastener schedule and spacing

Corrosion-resistant fasteners, at the height the product calls for, spaced to the schedule, snug and never countersunk into the face. Over-driven fasteners crack cement board; under-driven ones leave it loose to chatter.

Expansion gaps at every joint

Butt joints get a gap, and the gap gets the sealant or cover the product line specifies. Fiber cement moves less than wood or vinyl, but it still moves between a February night and a July afternoon.

Cut-edge treatment

Every field cut exposes raw substrate, and raw substrate drinks water. Cut ends get sealed or primed before the board goes up, ends stay clear of grade, roofs and decks, and cuts are made with dust control.

Trim, corners and reveals

HardieTrim corner posts and window and door surrounds, run plumb with a consistent reveal. Trim is part of the drainage layer, not decoration glued on afterwards.

Substrate repair before any of it

Rotted sheathing, soft rim joist, failed wrap and old flashing get dealt with first. Cement board over rot is a durable cover over a wall that keeps decaying.

Product lines

HardiePlank, HardieShingle, HardieTrim

  • HardiePlank lap siding — the horizontal lap most Columbus homes take. Exposure comes from the profile, and smooth versus wood-grain texture reads differently at twenty feet.
  • HardieShingle panels — staggered-edge panels for gables, dormers and accents, and the stand-in when a house wants a shake look without cedar's upkeep. See cedar shake siding and its substitutes.
  • HardieTrim — corners, fascia and window and door surrounds in the same cement substrate, so the trim does not rot out years before the field does.

Vertical layouts work in fiber cement too. Board and batten in Hardie, LP and vinyl compares how each substrate holds a straight line.

Finish

ColorPlus in the factory, or paint on site

ColorPlus factory finish

Color baked on under controlled conditions, so the film is even and the pigment is consistent board to board. It arrives finished, which shortens the job and keeps overspray off your brick and windows. The trade is the palette that exists.

Primed board, painted on site

The route when you want an exact color, when an HOA palette sits outside the factory range, or when body and trim should read as one finish. Cut ends and fastener heads get sealed during prep, which separates a finish that lasts from one that peels at the joints.

Either way, the color is chosen at your house, sample against the brick. A swatch on a screen is not a decision.

Honest limits

What fiber cement is not

It is heavy, so a strip and re-side carries real labor and disposal, and its cost band sits above vinyl and above most engineered wood. It is brittle before it is fastened, so boards crack if carried flat or dropped. It is not a structural repair: it will not fix a rotted rim joist, a failed wrap or a gutter that dumps onto the wall.

If the wall is sound and the budget is tight, insulated vinyl may be the sensible call and we will say so. If the house wants a lighter board that cuts faster on tall gables, LP SmartSide is the comparison worth having. If something is failing now, start on siding repair in Columbus or the siding hub.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about fiber cement

Does fiber cement handle an Ohio winter?

Yes, when it goes on with the gaps and flashing it wants. The board does not rot or split from freeze-thaw. The failure points are joints, cut ends and transitions.

Is it quieter and steadier than vinyl?

Rain on cement board reads duller than on vinyl, and the board does not expand enough to tick on a hot afternoon. What matters more behind it is the wrap and the insulation, which we handle in the same scope.

Can you match new fiber cement to siding already on the house?

Sometimes, and we will tell you which case you are in. Profile, exposure and color lot all have to line up. Matching siding that is no longer made covers what happens when they do not.
