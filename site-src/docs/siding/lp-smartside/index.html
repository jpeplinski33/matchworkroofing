LP SmartSide engineered wood

LP SmartSide, and where it beats cement board

A lighter board that cuts clean, holds a straight line and goes up faster on a tall Columbus wall. It is not the answer everywhere, and we will tell you where it is not.

Free estimate How we match siding to a house

What it is

Engineered wood, not the plywood your grandfather cursed

SmartSide is an engineered-wood substrate: wood strands bonded under heat and pressure into a panel, then treated with the SmartGuard process to resist fungal decay and termite attack, and finished with a factory primer that takes paint evenly.

The result behaves like wood where wood is good and stops behaving like wood where wood is bad. It holds a fastener, takes a crisp shadow line and does not split when you nail near an end. It is not solid lumber, so it does not cup or check like a green board.

It also weighs less per board than cement board, which changes the whole job on a two-story wall.

Where it wins

Four places SmartSide is the better call

Weight, on a tall wall

A lighter board is easier to carry up a ladder, easier to hold plumb while it is fastened and easier on the crew on a third-storey gable. Less dead load too, which matters on an older Columbus frame.

Cuttability around openings

It cuts with ordinary blades, so cutouts, mitred corners and gable angles go faster and come out cleaner. No silica dust on your driveway, no scored-and-snapped edges to seal.

Board and batten, straight

Vertical board and batten lives or dies on straight lines, and a stable engineered panel holds one better than a board that wants to move. See board and batten in Hardie, LP and vinyl for how the three compare.

Shake panel and accents

Panel profiles with a staggered, shake-like texture give a gable or a first-floor accent some depth without the maintenance of real cedar. The cedar argument itself is on cedar shake siding and its substitutes.

The honest comparison

Where fiber cement is the better call

This is not a rivalry. Both products are on our trucks, and the right one depends on your wall.

  • Splash-back and grade. Where a roof dumps onto a wall, or siding sits close to a patio, cement board tolerates the wetting better. Engineered wood needs clearances kept and cut ends sealed.
  • Impact. A cement board takes a thrown branch, a bicycle pedal or a stray trim board better than a wood-composite panel does.
  • Insects and decay. SmartGuard resists both, but on a house with carpenter ants at the rim joist, cement removes the food source entirely.
  • Neighbourhood consistency. On a Dublin or Powell street where every house is fiber cement, staying in that family reads better at resale.

The full cement-board case, including flashing and fastener detail, is on the James Hardie fiber cement page.

Whichever board you choose, the wrap, the flashing and the joint gap do the weatherproofing. Material choice is second to that.

Installation

How SmartSide gets installed here

  1. Substrate and wrap first

    Old siding off, rot cut out, sheathing checked, then the weather-resistive barrier lapped shingle-style with flashing at every head, sill and transition.

  2. Fasten to the schedule

    Corrosion-resistant fasteners into framing, at the spacing the product specifies, seated flat without crushing the panel face.

  3. Seal every cut end

    Field cuts expose raw substrate. Ends get sealed or primed on the ground before the board goes up, and panel ends stay clear of grade, roofs and decks.

  4. Leave the movement gap

    Butt joints and trim intersections get the gap the product wants, so a humid July does not push panels into a wave.

  5. Finish paint, top to bottom

    Factory primer, then a full finish coat over the field, the trim and every sealed cut. Colour chosen at the house against your brick and roof.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about SmartSide

Is engineered wood a bad idea in Ohio weather?

Not when it is detailed properly. The failures we open up are almost always unsealed cut ends, panels against grade, or a wall wrapped wrong behind them.

How often does it need paint?

On a schedule set by exposure, not by the calendar in a brochure. South and west faces in Columbus take the UV and go first. We write the finish system into the proposal so you know what you are maintaining.

Can you repair one section of it?

Usually, yes, and that is one of its advantages: panels cut out and patch in cleanly. The catch is colour lot and profile, which is the whole subject of matching siding that is no longer made.
