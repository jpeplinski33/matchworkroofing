Siding

Siding chosen for the house, not the pallet

Fiber cement and engineered wood first, picked against your brick, trim and roofline, then fastened the way the material wants. The reasoning sits on the Matchwork method.

Where we start

Two materials before anything else

Most siding failures we get called to are not material failures. They are installation failures, or a product that never suited the wall it went on. So we begin with the two that hold up on a central Ohio house — James Hardie fiber cement and LP SmartSide engineered wood — and choose between them at your elevation, in daylight, against the brick and the trim.

A Columbus wall takes freeze-thaw through February, wind-driven rain off Hoover Reservoir, straight-line wind in July and hard UV on a south face. Cement board shrugs off moisture and does not feed rot. Engineered wood is lighter, cuts cleaner and holds a crisp shadow line on a tall wall. Both are right answers on the right house.

Builder-grade vinyl is not where we start. We install it when it is the honest answer, and it is a weak one on a brick-front colonial or a wall with rot behind the sheathing.

How it runs

The order matters more than the brand

The same panel installed in the wrong order leaks, cups or cracks at the joint within a few Ohio seasons. This is the sequence on a full replacement.

  1. Measure and photograph the elevations

    Every wall, corner and opening. Where we suspect rot, a board comes off before we quote.

  2. Choose material, profile and color on the wall

    Samples against your brick at the hour the sun hits that elevation. Showroom decisions do not survive a Columbus house.

  3. Strip, repair the substrate, wrap

    Old siding off, rotted sheathing cut out, then the barrier and flashing at every penetration.

  4. Install to the fastener schedule

    Correct fastener, spacing and height in the panel, plus the gaps that let the wall move through winter.

  5. Trim, soffit and fascia last

    One finish pass, so the water path at the roof edge stays continuous with the wall.

A siding job is a drainage job with a finish on it. If the wrap, the flashing and the gaps are wrong, the panel color is the only thing that looks right.

Repairs and matching

A lot of siding work is not a replacement

Loose laps after a wind event, a panel cracked by a ladder, a corner post split by freeze-thaw, fascia rot behind a gutter that has overflowed for two winters. Those are repairs, and that is where matching becomes the whole job.

Siding repairs in Columbus is the repair menu across all four trades. Matching discontinued siding is the harder case, where the profile or color is no longer made. When an inspection names siding as a remedy item before closing, the realtor program covers how that work is documented for the file.

Common questions

What Columbus homeowners ask about siding

Should new siding go over the old siding?

We generally strip to the sheathing. Layering hides rot, hides a failed wrap and pushes every window, corner and trim detail out another thickness.

When is siding season in central Ohio?

Most of the year. Fiber cement and engineered wood install happily in cool weather; vinyl wants warmth, because it is brittle when cold and needs room to move in summer heat.

Does my HOA get a say in the color?

Often, yes. Dublin, Powell and New Albany communities keep approved palettes. We build the selection around that list, trim and accents included.
