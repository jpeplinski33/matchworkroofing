Chimney repair and rebuild

Chimney repair, crowns, caps and full rebuilds

A masonry chimney is the most exposed part of your house: above the roofline, weather from every direction, built from the materials least tolerant of it.

Free estimate How matching works

Diagnosis

What actually fails on a Columbus chimney

Cracked crown

The slab at the top is the chimney's roof. It cracks from freeze-thaw, water runs down inside the wall, and the first sign is spalling several courses below.

Open joints and spalling brick

Mortar fails before brick. Once a joint opens the brick behind it saturates, faces flake at the arris, and the chimney sheds fragments onto the roof.

Flashing at the roof line

The most common leak source, and the one that gets caulked instead of repaired: counterflashing pulled from its joint, step flashing flattened by movement.

Missing or failed cap

An open top lets rain, snow and nesting material straight in. A cap also keeps the crown from soaking from above, which stops freeze-thaw at its source.

Crown and cap

The two details that keep water out of the top

A crown is a sloped concrete slab that sheds water off the top of the wall, with an overhang and drip edge so runoff clears the brick face. It sits on a bond break so the slab can move without cracking the wall.

Cracked crowns get repaired where the concrete is sound and cast new where it is not.

A cap keeps rain, snow and nesting material out of the top. Caps come in copper, galvanized and stainless, and the metal matters: copper runoff streaks light brick, so we specify it against the masonry below. Same reasoning as copper and half-round gutters.

Where the chimney meets the roof

Step flashing, counterflashing and the cricket

Step flashing runs up the two slopes beside the chimney, one piece per course of shingles, lapped so water sheds down and out. It is woven into the shingle field, so it cannot be replaced properly without lifting the shingles around it. A caulked ladder repair is not a repair.

Counterflashing is cut into a reglet — a horizontal kerf in the mortar joint — then bent down over the step flashing and sealed. It stops water running down the brick face from getting behind the flashing. Where the kerf was never cut and the counterflashing was caulked to the surface, the chimney leaks on the windward side in driving rain.

Base flashing and the cricket. At the low side, a cricket or saddle diverts water around the stack instead of letting it pond behind. On a chimney broad enough to dam the flow this is not optional.

We do both sides as one job: roof-side step flashing under roof leak and flashing repair, masonry-side reglet and counterflashing here. Splitting it between a roofer and a mason is how the leak survives both.

Waterproofing

Sealers, and the ones that make it worse

A chimney can be waterproofed, but only with a vapor-permeable silane or siloxane sealer that repels liquid water while letting vapor out. Applied to sound, repaired masonry it buys the wall years.

We will not apply a film-forming coating or elastomeric paint over a failing chimney. Those trap moisture inside the brick: the wall looks better for a season, then spalls behind the coating.

Order matters: crown and cap first, joints repointed, brick replaced, then sealer over the dry wall.

Rebuilds

Full rebuild, including during a roof replacement

When the stack leans, the brick is spalled through, the crown is broken into sections or repairs keep failing, the answer is a rebuild. Above the roof line that means taking the chimney down to sound brick, recasting the crown, resetting the cap and rebuilding the courses in brick matched to the original.

Below the roof line the two trades have to be sequenced. If the roof is being replaced, the chimney comes down to the deck while the shingles are off, the flashing is rebuilt into fresh masonry, and the roof goes back around a sound stack. Doing the chimney afterward means cutting into a new roof.

Tear-off sequencing is described on the roof replacement page, brick sourcing on brick matching and repair.

Scope

What we do on a chimney, and what we do not

We repair, rebuild, crown, cap, flash, counterflash, repoint, replace brick and waterproof masonry chimneys, roof-side integration included. Liner work and cleaning are outside our scope, and we say so plainly.

Wider envelope work sits under our masonry hub, localized fixes under the masonry section of our exterior repairs page, and matching follows the Matchwork method.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Common questions

Chimney questions

Water on my ceiling near the chimney. Where is it coming from?

Usually the counterflashing or the crown, not where the stain appears. Water travels along the wall before it drops, so the stain is rarely the source.

Is my chimney unsafe to use?

We assess masonry condition, lean and flashing, and will tell you if a stack is unsound. Anything affecting safe operation goes to the trade that handles it, which is not us.

Should I do the chimney with a new roof?

If it needs work, yes. The flashing gets rebuilt into the roof either way, and doing the stack while the shingles are off beats cutting into a finished roof later.
