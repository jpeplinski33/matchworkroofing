Architectural shingles

Architectural, designer and Class 4 shingles

Three conversations that get lumped into one word: what the shingle is, what the impact rating means, and what it looks like from your street.

Free estimate How matching works

The baseline

What an architectural shingle actually is

Two layers of shingle bonded into one, with a cut-out pattern on the top layer. That is the whole trick, and it does a lot of work.

A three-tab is a single flat layer with slots cut in it, so the roof reads as a grid of identical rectangles. An architectural shingle is laminated: a full base layer with a second, partial layer bonded on top. The result is thickness at the tab, a hard edge that throws a shadow, and a granule blend that varies from shingle to shingle instead of repeating visibly up the slope.

That shadow line is what your eye reads first from the curb, which is why a correct color on a flat three-tab still looks cheap. Lamination is also structural: more material per shingle means more nail-holding area, better resistance to a tab lifting in wind, and a mat that takes an impact without bruising straight through.

Above architectural sit the designer profiles — heavier, multi-layer shingles that read as slate or wood from the street — and the impact-resistant products, which are a separate question from profile.

  • Laminated and dimensional, thicker at the tab than a three-tab.
  • Granule color blended across the field rather than one flat tone.
  • A shadow line that reads from the street before the color does.
  • More nail-holding area, so a tab is harder for wind to lift.
  • Laid to a stated exposure, which keeps the course lines straight.

We install CertainTeed shingles. Our residential baseline is CertainTeed Landmark PRO, with Landmark below it, Landmark Premium above it, and Presidential at the heavier, slate-profile end of the catalog.

Impact resistance

What Class 4 actually means, and where it pays off here

Class 4 is the top of a four-step impact scale, earned by a specific test on a specific shingle — not by a brand or a price.

The classification comes from the industry's steel-ball impact test. A steel ball is dropped onto the shingle twice, onto the same spot, and the scale runs from Class 1 to Class 4 by ball size. Class 4 uses a two-inch ball dropped from twenty feet. Afterwards the shingle is turned over and inspected from underneath: to pass, there can be no cracking, splitting, fracturing, tearing or puncture of the mat visible on the back.

So Class 4 is not a durability guarantee and it says nothing about how many years a roof will last. It is a statement about one thing: this shingle survived a defined impact in a lab, on the underside of the mat, without breaking. A Class 4 shingle still loses granules, still ages in UV, and still fails at a flashing that was never right.

Where it pays off in central Ohio is hail. The storms that matter here are rarely the golf-ball ones that make the news. They are mid-size hail arriving with a July derecho-grade front, hitting a south and west slope hard, and leaving bruising in the mat rather than a hole you can see from the ground. Bruising shortens a shingle's life because the mat under the granules has been crushed and the granules over that spot let go early. A Class 4 shingle is built to take that hit without bruising through.

Hail bruising

The failure Class 4 is aimed at: crushed mat, early granule loss over the impact, a shortened slope life.

South and west slopes

UV softens the asphalt over years and a softened mat bruises more easily. Those two slopes take the worst of both.

Ridges and hips

Exposed corners catch hail at an angle. The matching hip and ridge cap is part of the system, not an afterthought.

CertainTeed makes impact-resistant versions of its architectural lines. Which are current, which colors they come in and which suit your roof are things we show you at the house, because the lineup moves. We will not name a rating we cannot put in writing on the proposal.

Homeowners sometimes ask whether a Class 4 roof changes their premium. We do not know and we will not guess. That is a question for your agent, not your roofer.

The rest of the system

A shingle is one component of five

Wind ratings, algae resistance and warranty coverage all depend on parts you cannot see once the roof is finished.

CertainTeed's Landmark products carry a wind-resistance warranty up to 110 mph, and up to 160 mph when the roof is built with CertainTeed-matched starter shingles and hip and ridge cap. That upgrade condition is the point: the wind rating belongs to the assembled system, not to the shingle on a pallet.

So every roof goes on as a complete CertainTeed Integrity Roof System — matched underlayment, starter shingles, field shingles, hip and ridge accessories and ventilation, specified together. On a Columbus house with mature trees and a north slope we also talk about StreakFighter algae resistance, because dark streaking on a shaded north face is a real aesthetic failure and it is addressed in the shingle rather than by a power washer later.

Underneath: ice-and-water shield at eaves, valleys and penetrations, synthetic underlayment across the deck, a clean drip edge and balanced ridge ventilation. That stack is described on the full roof replacement page.

Warranty

  • Manufacturing defects on the complete roofing system

Extended coverage is tied to the system being installed as a system: CertainTeed underlayment, starter, shingles, hip and ridge, and ventilation, on a roof torn off rather than layered over. Roof-overs do not qualify.

The exact coverage available on your roof, what it includes and how long it runs are written into your proposal before you sign anything.

Ohio does not issue a statewide roofing license; roofing contractors register with the municipalities where they work. That is why a manufacturer credential is worth naming at all — it is an independent measure of training that has nothing to do with a marketing badge.

Choosing the colorway

Blend, not name

An architectural shingle is not one color. It is a blend of granule colors in a repeating pattern, and two products that both read as weathered wood from the street can be built from different blends. Against a Georgian red brick the wrong blend goes plum; against a warm limestone it goes flat and gray.

So full-size boards come to your house and get held against the brick at the gable, the fascia and soffit, the garage door and the shutters, then looked at again in different light. Where there is a review board or an approved association list, that gets checked before the order goes in. The roofing-specific detail, including what happens when only part of a roof is being touched, is on the shingle matching page.

  • Full-size sample boards, at your house, in your own daylight.
  • Held against brick, stone, siding, fascia, trim and shutters.
  • Looked at twice, in different light, before anything is ordered.
  • Photographed so the comparison is on the record.
  • Association and review-board lists checked in advance.

Nobody picks a roof color off a phone screen with us. If the answer needs a second visit, it takes a second visit.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about shingles

Is architectural the same as dimensional?

Yes. Architectural, dimensional and laminated all describe one construction: a base layer with a second partial layer bonded on top. The names are marketing; the build is the point.

Does Class 4 mean hail cannot damage the roof?

No. It means the shingle survived a defined steel-ball impact in a lab without breaking the mat. Large enough hail, at the right angle, on a shingle that has baked in UV for a decade, will still do damage.

Should I go heavier than architectural?

On an estate-scale house with long rake lines, a heavier designer profile is often right. On a small ranch it can read busy, and the extra weight has to be carried by the decking, which we answer before we recommend anything.

If your shingles are failing at one detail rather than across the field, that is a repair. Our roofing repairs in Columbus cover leaks, flashing, vents and the small stuff, and we will tell you which one you have.
