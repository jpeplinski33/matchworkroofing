Where the work starts

The house is picked before the roof is measured

A shingle name and a two-inch chip tell you almost nothing about how a roof will read from your street.

Central Ohio housing stock was built to a palette: Georgian brick in New Albany, repeating builder colorways in Powell, Tudor Revivals in Upper Arlington, farmhouse stock around Westerville. Put a roof on one of those houses without looking at the house and it reads as an addition, however well it was installed.

So we start with the house. Full-size sample boards go up against your brick, stone, siding, fascia and shutters, in the light your building actually sits in. Then we measure. Then we write the scope. Then we order. The long version of that reasoning is on the Matchwork Method page.

By request

Flat, low-slope, HOA and church roofs

Not every roof here is a pitched shingle roof. Porch and addition roofs sit nearly flat, mid-century stock was built low-slope on purpose, and association buildings and churches need a membrane plus an access and phasing plan a single-family re-roof never has.

We take that work by request rather than leading with it, because it is a small part of what we do and deserves its own detail.

  • Flat and low-slope roofing: EPDM, TPO, PVC and modified bitumen
  • Multifamily, HOA, church and small commercial roofing
  • Standing seam metal as a porch, bay or dormer accent

When weather is the reason you called

Storm damage, documented calmly

July straight-line wind, winter freeze-thaw, and hail that shows up as bruising in the mat rather than as a hole you can see from the ground.

If a storm is why you are here: we inspect, mark a test square on each slope, count and photograph what is inside it, and write down what we found. If a claim is filed, we walk the roof with your adjuster so the same facts are in front of both of you. We do not promise an outcome, and most of the roofs we install are planned replacements with no claim attached at all.

Common questions

What Columbus homeowners ask us first

Do I need a repair or a replacement?

That is decided on the roof, not on the phone. We look at the shingle mat, the flashings, the deck from the attic side, and the age of the field. Sometimes the honest answer is a repair and a slope to watch.

Why is there no price list here?

Area, pitch, facet count, deck condition and material all move the number, sometimes widely between two houses on one street. We measure yours and hand you a written, itemized proposal instead.

Is roofing all you do?

No. Siding, gutters and masonry run through the same crew, which matters where a chimney flashing, a gutter run and a shingle field meet at one corner. Start at exterior repairs and give us the whole list.
