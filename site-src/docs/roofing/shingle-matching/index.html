Shingle matching

Matching a roof is three things, not one

Color, texture and profile. Get two of the three right and the roof still looks like it belongs to a different house. It is the whole reason the company is called what it is.

What we compare

Color, texture, profile

A shingle name and a swatch chip tell you almost nothing. These three do.

Color, and the blend inside it

An architectural shingle is not one color. It is a blend of granule colors laid in a repeating pattern, and two products that read as the same weathered wood from the street can be built from completely different blends. We look at the blend, not the name on the wrapper.

Texture and shadow line

How deep the laminate sits, how hard an edge the tab throws, how coarse the granule is. Shadow line is what your eye reads first from the curb, which is why a correct color on the wrong texture still looks patched.

Profile and exposure

Tab width, cut-out spacing and the exposure the shingle is laid at. Change the exposure by a fraction of an inch across a slope and the course lines no longer line up with the roof beside them. That is visible from the street forever.

The house, not the catalog

We match to your brick, your trim and your light

A roof is the largest single surface on the front of your house, and it has to sit under a specific brick, beside a specific trim color, in a specific direction of light. A shingle that looks right in a supplier's showroom can turn plum against red brick, or go flat and gray against a warm limestone.

So we bring full-size boards to the house and hold them where they will actually live: against the brick or stone at the gable, next to the fascia and soffit, beside the garage door and the shutters. Then we do it twice, once in morning light and once with the sun round the back, because the same granule blend shifts through the day.

If your neighborhood has a review board or an association with an approved list, that gets checked before the order goes in, not after the pallets are on the drive.

  • Full-size sample boards, at your house, in your own daylight.
  • Held against brick, stone, siding, fascia, trim and shutters.
  • Looked at twice, in different light, before anything is ordered.
  • Photographed so the comparison is on the record and not just in memory.
  • Association or review-board lists checked in advance where they apply.

Nobody chooses a roof color off a phone screen with us. If the answer takes a second visit, it takes a second visit.

Partial roofs and additions

When only part of the roof is being touched

A new sunroom, a re-shingled garage, a single slope after a tree came down. Matching is hardest here, and it is worth being straight about why.

  • An existing roof has weathered. A brand-new shingle out of the wrapper has not, and it will not catch up for years.
  • Granule blends are reformulated over time, so the same product name from a different year can be a visibly different roof.
  • Product lines get discontinued. When yours has, the question stops being which one matches and becomes which one is closest, and where the line between old and new should fall.

What we can do is put the new work where the eye forgives it: break at a ridge, a valley or a natural change of plane rather than mid-slope, and choose the closest available blend rather than the closest name.

What we will not do is tell you a partial roof will be invisible when we can see that it will not be. You get shown the two shingles side by side on your own roof, and you decide whether the difference is one you can live with.

Sometimes the honest recommendation is to leave a slope alone for now. We would rather say that than sell a repair you will look at every time you pull into the drive.

If insurance is involved

What a shingle match test actually is

Calmly, and without any promise about how a claim will end.

When a storm damages part of a roof, the question of whether the undamaged part can be matched sometimes comes up. A shingle match test is how that question is answered with evidence instead of opinion.

A small sample is taken from an inconspicuous area of your existing roof and sent to an independent testing laboratory. The lab identifies the product by its physical characteristics and reports what it is, and whether that product is still available. The report comes back as a document. It states findings about the shingle, and nothing about your policy.

We take the sample, seal the test area behind us, submit it, and hand you the result whether or not it says what anyone hoped it would say.

What the test is not

  • It is not a decision. Your carrier decides what your policy covers, and a lab report is one input among several.
  • It is not a guarantee of any outcome, and anyone telling you it is has told you something they cannot know.
  • It is not a substitute for the inspection. Damage still has to be documented on its own terms.

If a claim is already open, send what you have to claims@matchworkroofing.com and we will tell you plainly whether a match test is even worth doing on your roof. Often it is not.

Most Matchwork roofs are planned replacements with no claim attached at all. Matching is a craft question first and a claims question a distant second. More on the claims side is on our storm damage and insurance claims page.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about matching

Can't you just look up the color name on the old shingle?

Sometimes a name is legible on the underside of a tab, and it helps narrow the search. It does not finish the job. Blends get reformulated, product lines get renamed and discontinued, and a name from a decade ago can point at a shingle that looks nothing like the one now on your roof. The name is a starting point and the boards at your house are the answer.

Can I send you a photo and a sample instead?

A photo is useful for context and a loose shingle or a cut-off tab is genuinely helpful, because we can look at its blend and its laminate. Neither one replaces being at the house. A photograph carries the camera's white balance, not your brick, and a shingle pulled from a north slope has weathered differently from the one on the south.

Will new shingles blend in with the old ones eventually?

They will close some of the gap and they will not close all of it. A new shingle out of the wrapper has full granule brightness and no UV history. It weathers over a few seasons, which is why we place the break at a ridge, a valley or a change of plane rather than mid-slope, so the difference sits where the eye already expects a line.

My association has an approved color list. What then?

Then the list gets checked before anything is ordered, not after the pallets are on the drive. Where the approved options do not include anything that suits the house, we will say so and help you put the case to the review board with samples and photographs rather than a description.

What does a match test cost, and who pays for it?

We do not put figures on this page, because the answer depends on what is being tested and who has asked for it. What we will do is tell you before any sample leaves the roof whether a test is worth doing at all on your particular roof, what it can and cannot establish, and who it is for. Often the honest answer is that it is not worth doing.

Is a match test the same as a roof inspection?

No. A test identifies a product from a physical sample and reports whether it is still available. An inspection documents the condition of the roof: the field, the flashings, the penetrations, the deck and the ventilation. One is about identity, the other about state, and a claim that involves both needs both. The inspection is described on the roof inspection page.
