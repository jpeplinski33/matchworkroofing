Cedar shake

Cedar shake, and what it takes to keep it

We install it, we repair it, and we will tell you what a wood roof asks of a homeowner in central Ohio before you choose one rather than after.

Free estimate How matching works

The material

Shakes are split, shingles are sawn

Two products called by one name. A shake is split from a bolt of cedar and is thicker and rougher; a shingle is sawn and is thinner and smoother.

Both are laid in overlapping courses with a deliberate gap between units, so the wood can swell when wet and shrink when dry without buckling the field. Underneath runs an interlay — felt or synthetic between courses — because the gaps are open by design and the interlay is what sheds the water. A cedar roof is a drained and vented assembly, not a sealed one, so airflow behind the wood matters as much as the wood itself. Cedar laid tight to a solid deck stays wet, and a cedar roof that stays wet fails early.

Western red cedar weathers from a warm tone to silver-gray over a few seasons, unevenly: faster on a south slope in full sun, slower under tree cover on a north face.

The honest part

What it takes to keep it

Cedar is not a fire-and-forget roof. It is a material that wants to be dry, and Ohio shade does a lot to stop it being dry.

Moss, algae and lichen

Under mature trees, on a north slope, or anywhere the roof stays damp, moss colonizes the butt of the shake and lifts it. Once a shake is held up, water runs under it instead of over it and the interlay starts taking weather. This is the most common way a cedar roof in this metro dies young.

Debris, drainage and airflow

Leaves in the valleys and needle litter behind chimneys keep the wood wet for days after rain, and a gutter that overflows wets the rake and fascia from below — see the gutters hub. Behind the shakes, air has to move: a vented attic and balanced intake and exhaust keep the back of the wood dry, where rot starts unseen.

Curling, cupping and splitting

Cedar moves. Butts curl as the top face dries faster than the bottom, cups across the grain, and splits along it as freeze-thaw works the wood. A few curled or split units are a repair — shakes replaced individually with a cedar bib and a stainless fastener. A field where most units are curled is finished, and no treatment brings it back.

Cleaning, gently, and treatments

Moss comes off mechanically with a soft rinse, from the ridge down — never a high-pressure wand, which strips the soft latewood out of the grain and shortens what is left. Water-repellent and moss-inhibiting treatments help, but they wear off and need reapplication. A preservative on a roof that stays wet under maples is money spent slowly.

The question everyone asks

Fire, and what to ask about coverage

Cedar is wood. It is a combustible roofing material, and it would be dishonest to describe it at length without saying so.

Pressure-impregnated fire-retardant cedar products exist and are a different article from untreated shakes: treated through the thickness of the wood rather than coated on it. That means site cutting, fasteners and any replacement shakes added later all have to be handled the same way, or the assembly is no longer what was rated.

Some municipalities treat wood roofing differently from other materials in their building and fire requirements, and property policies treat wood roofs differently from one another. Both vary by jurisdiction and by carrier, and neither is something a roofing contractor can answer for you.

The honest version: before you commit to cedar, ask your agent two questions in writing — whether the property is eligible for coverage with a wood roof on it, and whether a fire-retardant treated product changes that answer. Keep the reply with the proposal. We will build whatever you decide, and we will name the exact product and treatment we install so the answer you were given applies to the roof you get. We will not guess at what an insurer will do, and we will not promise you one outcome or the other.

  • Untreated cedar is a combustible roofing material.
  • Fire-retardant cedar is pressure-impregnated, not surface-coated.
  • Cutting, fasteners and later replacement shakes have to match what was rated.
  • Municipal requirements and property policies both vary; neither is ours to answer.
  • Ask your agent in writing before you order, and keep the reply.

Where cedar belongs here

Estate stock, and the alternative worth knowing about

In this metro cedar shows up mostly on estate-scale houses — New Albany and the communities around it, where lots carry mature tree cover and the architecture was drawn for a heavy, textured roof. On those houses cedar is right and nothing else reads the same way.

On a house with tight tree cover, a shaded north slope, or an owner who does not want a maintenance cycle, we will say so and put the alternative in front of you: designer shingle profiles built to read as wood shake from the street, with a heavy laminate and a deep shadow line and none of the moss problem. They are not cedar and we will not pretend they are. That conversation is on the architectural, designer and Class 4 shingle page, and the neighbourhood itself on roofing and exterior work in New Albany.

  • Shakes or shingles sorted on site for tone, taper and thickness.
  • Interlay between courses, and an air gap behind the wood where the roof needs it.
  • Stainless or hot-dipped fasteners — plain steel stains cedar black and lets go early.
  • Open valleys in copper or a compatible metal, detailed so debris does not dam.
  • Flashing at chimneys, sidewalls and dormers cut and counterflashed, not sealed over.

Common questions

What homeowners ask about cedar

How much maintenance is this really?

On a shaded, tree-covered site: a look every year, debris cleared from the valleys, and moss dealt with before it lifts the butts. On an open, sunny site with good airflow, considerably less. Exposure decides the cycle more than anything we can do at install.

Can you repair part of a cedar roof?

Yes, and it is usually the right answer. Individual shakes get pulled and replaced with new cedar, and a weathered field blends them in over a season or two. What we will not do is tell you a repair fixes a field curling across the whole slope.

Should I have it pressure washed?

No. High-pressure water strips the soft grain out of the wood, raises the surface fibres and opens the shake to more moss, faster. Anyone offering to blast a cedar roof clean is offering to shorten it.
