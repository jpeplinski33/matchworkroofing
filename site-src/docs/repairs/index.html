How a repair starts

We find the entry point before we quote anything

Water travels. The stain on the ceiling is rarely directly under the hole in the roof.

Every repair begins the same way: eyes on the failure, the water path traced back to where it got in, and a note of what is damaged versus what is only old. On a central Ohio house the entry point is a detail, not a field — a pipe boot, a step-flashing course, counterflashing cut into brick, an open gutter seam, a spalled mortar joint.

Then you get a written scope: the repair, the material, the colour we intend to match, and what we are not touching. If a repair is throwing money after a failing system, we say so. If the list came out of a home inspection during a sale, our Close-Ready realtor program handles the documentation side.

Roofing

Roofing repairs: flashing and the courses around it

Most leaks we are called to are not worn-out shingles. They are a detail installed once, quickly, never revisited. We pull back the courses above the failure, look at the deck, and rebuild the detail in order instead of caulking over it.

  • Step flashing replaced course by course, not bent and re-used
  • Counterflashing recut into the mortar joint at brick walls and chimneys
  • Pipe boots and vent collars, which fail long before the shingle does
  • Valley metal re-formed where water has cut under a course
  • Wind-lifted tabs re-sealed, and creased shingles documented
  • Ice-dam damage at the eaves, with shield added where there was none
  • Soft deck cut out and re-decked before anything is covered back up

More on roof leak and flashing repair and skylight replacement and curb flashing; the trade starts at the Columbus roofing hub. When repair is no longer the answer we scope a full roof replacement instead. Chimney flashing is two trades: ours is the flashing, the masonry is on chimney repair and rebuild.

When the roof is open and weather is coming

We tarp the exposed section and dry what we can reach, so the damage does not keep growing while the permanent repair is being scheduled.

Siding

Siding repairs: the wall behind the panel matters more

Vinyl cracks at a corner post where a ladder leaned. Fiber cement chips at the bottom course where splash-back wicks up for years. Trim rots from the end grain inward; soffit and fascia rot behind an overflowing gutter. We pull enough panel to see sheathing and wrap, cut out what is soft, rebuild the water path, put the siding back.

  • Fascia and soffit rot cut out, gutter mounting surface rebuilt behind it
  • Trim, corner posts, J-channel and surrounds, with end grain sealed
  • Cracked or wind-lifted panels replaced at the same profile and exposure
  • Kickout flashing added where a roof slope ends at a wall
  • Discontinued siding tracked down, or matched as far as it allows

Matching is the hard half of a partial repair, and the subject of discontinued siding matching. Rot and trim work is on soffit, fascia and trim repair, materials on the Columbus siding hub.

Gutters

Gutter repairs: pitch, seams and where the water goes next

Gutters fail in three places, in order: seams open, fasteners pull out of fascia, and downspouts stop moving water far enough from the foundation. Overflow at a corner is usually pitch, not capacity. A run that pulls away in winter is usually a spike in rotted fascia.

  • Failed seams and end caps cut out and replaced, not smeared with sealant
  • Pitch corrected, hidden hangers set into solid blocking
  • Downspouts resized or added where one outlet carries two planes
  • Extensions and underground tie-ins that move water off the foundation
  • Colour matched to trim, brick or roof line, so a new run reads old

Below the gutter, drainage is on downspout extensions and underground tie-ins. Past-repair runs get seamless aluminum gutters, which remove the leaking seams. Gutter guards we talk you out of as often as into. The trade starts at the Columbus gutter hub.

Masonry

Masonry repairs: mortar, chimney and the roof transition

Brick outlives the mortar holding it. That is by design, and it is why the mortar is the repair.

Central Ohio masonry fails from water and freeze-thaw, almost never from load. A sandy joint lets water in, the water freezes, the face spalls, the next joint opens. A cracked crown does the same from above. We repair the masonry and the transition where it meets the roof, because that is where chimney leaks enter.

  • Tuckpointing with mortar matched on colour, sand and joint profile
  • Spalled brick replaced to the colour of age, not the colour of new
  • Chimney crown rebuilt or sealed, cap fitted where there is none
  • Flashing and counterflashing rebuilt into the joint from the roof side
  • Stucco and synthetic stucco, with kickout flashing at the roof

Chimneys: chimney repair, crowns, caps and rebuilds. Mortar: tuckpointing and mortar matching. Units: brick matching and repair. Walls: stucco repair and roof-to-wall flashing. The Columbus masonry hub covers the envelope.

Matching

A repair you can see from the street is not finished

A patch is easy. A patch that disappears is the job. Shingle colour, granule texture and shadow blend; siding profile and sheen; gutter colour against trim and brick; mortar tooling against brick weathered for decades. Each is a separate problem, solved with a physical sample held against your house in the light it sits in.

Sometimes the material is no longer made. Then you hear what can be matched, what cannot, and what the alternatives look like before anything is ordered. We do not promise invisibility on a weathered surface. The method is on the Matchwork Method, roofing match tests on shingle matching.

Storm damage

When the repair list came from a storm

Wind, hail and summer straight-line storms produce a different scope than wear: creased tabs, bruised mat, blown-off ridge caps, split siding, bent gutters, masonry off the top of a chimney. We document it with dated photographs, a test square where hail is in question, and a scope an adjuster can read.

Two things we will not do. We will not tell you a claim will be approved — nobody outside your carrier can. And we will not present a repair as a way around filing one: if the damage is storm damage, the honest route is to run the claim properly. You choose your contractor. The process is on storm damage and insurance claims.

Most Matchwork work has no claim attached to it. Wear, a bad original detail or a ladder is ordinary scoped work, and that is most of this page.
