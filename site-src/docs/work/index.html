What this page will hold

Real houses, real materials, real dates

Every entry here will be a job Matchwork performed, photographed on site by the crew that did it. Each one will say what the trade was, what the house is — brick foursquare, Tudor, colonial, ranch, split-level — what was failing, what we did, and what the new material was matched to. Roofing, siding, gutters and masonry are grouped separately, because a gutter run and a tuckpointing job are not the same kind of evidence.

What you will not find is a photograph we did not take, a house we did not work on, a street name invented to sound local, or a storm date attached to a scheduled replacement. A gallery that cannot be trusted is worse than none, so this one starts empty and fills slowly.

How the work is documented

What a completed job record contains

  1. The written scope, before work starts

    What is being repaired or replaced, with what material, in what colour or profile, and what is being left alone. Signed off before the first course comes off.

  2. Dated before photographs

    The failure as we found it: the stain, the lifted flashing, the open seam, the spalled joint, the rotted fascia. Wide enough to show the elevation, tight enough to show the defect.

  3. Dated during photographs

    The layers that get covered and can never be inspected again — bare deck, shield, underlayment, the wrap behind siding, flashing at a transition, a joint before it is tooled.

  4. Dated after photographs, same position

    The finished work shot from the same angle as the before photograph, so the change is visible without anybody taking our word for it.

  5. The paperwork that goes with it

    Materials and colours used, the details rebuilt, the repair-only warranty, and a completion letter where a transaction needs one. Gallery entries are drawn from that record.

The honest state of this page

There are no photographs here yet

The panels above are labelled shot briefs, not pictures. They describe what will be photographed, on what kind of central Ohio house, with what materials, so you can see the standard before a single image is published. When a job is finished, photographed and released by the homeowner, the brief is replaced with the photograph it describes and the entry goes up with its date, its trade and its scope.

In the meantime the substance of how we work is on pages that do not need photographs: the Matchwork Method for matching across four trades, exterior repairs in Columbus for what gets repaired and how, and the realtor repair program for what goes into a transaction file.
