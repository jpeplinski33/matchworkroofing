How we work

An owner on the roof, not just on the paperwork

Matchwork is small on purpose, and it works on four trades: roofing, siding, gutters and masonry. The person who stands in your driveway holding sample boards against your brick is there the morning the work starts.

Free estimate What happens, in order

Owner-led

One person is answerable for your house

Most exterior work goes wrong in the handoffs. A salesperson measures, a coordinator schedules, a crew nobody introduced you to arrives at seven in the morning, and by the time something is off nobody remembers what was promised on the driveway.

With four trades on one house the handoffs multiply. The roofer blames the gutter installer for a backed-up downspout. The siding crew says the flashing detail was not theirs. The mason points at the counterflashing the roofer set. Nobody is wrong about their own ten feet, and your wall still leaks.

We run it the other way. An owner does the inspection, writes the proposal, orders the material, is on site when the work starts, and comes back for the final walk. If a decision has to be made on the roof at eleven in the morning, it is made by someone who was in the first conversation and has looked at the wall below it.

  • You meet the person doing the estimate, and that person stays on the job.
  • Questions get answered by someone who has been on your roof, not from a script.
  • Changes are priced and written down before anyone acts on them.
  • Every crew on your house is scheduled, supervised and warranted by Matchwork, and you know who is coming before they arrive.
  • The trade boundaries inside a detail — roof to wall, gutter to fascia, chimney to shingle — are ours to resolve, not yours to referee.

We take fewer jobs at a time than we could. That is the trade we made, and it is why the paragraphs above hold.

Four trades, one contract

Why one contract for the whole envelope matters

A house does not fail in one trade. Water gets in at the chimney, runs behind the siding, rots the fascia and dumps out of a gutter against the footing.

When four separate companies touch that sequence, each sees a slice. The diagnosis stops at the edge of whoever is standing there, and the repair that gets sold is the one that company performs.

One company holding the whole scope sees that sequence at once, which changes four practical details: one measurement, one color decision, one schedule, and one person accountable afterwards. They are the reason a proposal for a roof, a wall and a gutter run reads as one document instead of three that disagree.

The matching half of that thinking has its own page, because it deserves more room than an about page gives it: the Matchwork method.

One measurement

Roof planes and pitch; wall elevations and what is behind them; gutter runs and where the water goes; chimney, crown and mortar joints. One visit, one person, so the numbers in your proposal agree with each other.

One color decision

Shingle to brick, siding to trim, gutter to the palette, mortar to an aged wall. Those decisions interact. Made together at your house in daylight they hold; made separately by four suppliers they argue for as long as you own the house.

One schedule

Tear-off, deck repair, flashing, siding, gutter hang and masonry happen in the order the weather and the water want, not the order four companies became available. The proposal states the sequence before you sign.

One person accountable

When the drip edge, gutter apron, fascia and counterflashing are all ours, there is no third party to point at afterwards. If a detail we built leaks, we own the whole detail and come back to it.

The process

What happens, in order

Five steps, no surprises between them.

  1. We look at the whole house

    Roof planes, valleys, step and counter flashing, chimney, pipe boots, drip edge, gutters and downspouts, siding and the substrate behind it, soffit and fascia, lintels and sills, and the attic if it can be reached. We photograph what we find and tell you what is wrong, including when the honest answer is that you have time left.

  2. We match the color in person, at your house

    Shingle boards, siding laps, gutter sections and mortar samples come out of the truck and go up against your brick, siding, stone and trim in your own daylight. Nobody picks an exterior color from a phone screen or a two-inch swatch. We look at it in morning light and again with the sun behind the house, because the same shingle reads brown at nine and gray at four.

  3. You get a written proposal

    One document, in plain English: the measurements, the exact product line and color, underlayment and ice-and-water coverage, ventilation, the flashing we are replacing, siding substrate and fastener details, gutter size and downspout routing, masonry scope and mortar match, and what we are not doing. Manufacturer warranty options are explained at your consultation. Nothing is priced verbally.

  4. The install, with the house protected

    Landscaping covered before the first shingle comes off. Plywood on the windows below the work when a tear-off is heavy. Beds and paths protected while scaffolding goes up. Deck inspected and re-nailed as needed, rotten sheathing shown to you before it is covered up, and soft masonry kept damp and out of the sun while it cures.

  5. Daily clean-up and a final walk

    You should be able to let a child or a dog into the yard the evening of a work day. At the end we walk the property together, look at the gutters, the ground, the ridge line and the flashings, and you get photographs and paperwork in one place.

Crew standards

What we hold the crew to, in every trade

The details nobody sees

New drip edge, new pipe boots, step flashing replaced rather than reused, valleys done to the manufacturer's detail, nails in the nailing zone and driven flush. On a wall: a drained and flashed weather-resistive barrier, correct fastener spacing, expansion gaps at trim. In masonry: the right mortar for the age of the brick, tooled joints, no silicone over a spalled face. The invisible work decides how long it lasts.

Your property comes first

Beds and shrubs covered, debris tarped rather than dropped, mortar splatter cleaned off brick the same day, and the driveway swept and run with a magnet every evening. The house should look better at six than it did at seven.

Nothing gets buried

Rotten decking, an old leak, a failed wrap behind siding, a rusted lintel, a flashing detail wrong from the day it was built: you see a photograph before it goes back under anything, and you decide what happens next.

Matching

The part of the work we are known for

Matching is not a color chip held up at arm's length. It is shingle to brick, siding to trim, gutter to the palette, and mortar to a wall that has weathered for decades. It is also knowing when a match is not available — a discontinued siding profile, a reformulated shingle blend, a brick no longer fired — and saying so instead of promising invisibility. How we work through it, and what the honest limits are, is on that page.

Materials and manufacturer coverage

Where the shingles come from

We install CertainTeed roofing systems as a complete system: shingle, underlayment, starter, hip and ridge, and ventilation from the same line, because that is what the manufacturer's coverage is written around.

Matchwork Roofing is a CertainTeed ShingleMaster credentialed contractor, which is what allows CertainTeed's extended SureStart PLUS warranty coverage to be offered on qualifying installations.

The exact warranty tier, what it covers, what it excludes and how long it runs are written into your proposal before you sign anything. We would rather show you the document than describe it on a web page.
