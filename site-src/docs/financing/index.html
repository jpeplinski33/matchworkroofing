Financing

Paying for a roof over time

A roof is rarely a planned purchase. Financing is arranged through an outside lending partner, and the terms come from that partner in writing, before you commit to anything.

Free estimate The order things happen in

How it works

We build roofs. Someone else does the lending.

Financing is offered through an independent lending partner. Matchwork is not a lender, does not make credit decisions, and does not set rates. The agreement, if you enter one, is between you and that lender.

That separation matters. It means nobody on our side has a reason to steer you toward a bigger scope than your house needs, and it means the terms you are offered come from a company whose business is lending, not roofing.

It also means the two conversations stay separate in time. The roof is measured, matched and priced first, in a written proposal you can read at your own table. Only then does the question of how to pay for it come up, with real numbers from the lender rather than an advertisement on a web page.

Manufacturer warranty options and financing are explained at your consultation.

Why there are no numbers on this page

Rates, terms and offers change, and what any one household qualifies for is not something a web page can know. Quoting a figure here that turns out not to apply to you would be worse than quoting nothing.

So we do it the slow way. You get the written proposal first, with the scope and the price settled. Then you ask the lender, or we raise it at the consultation, and you read what they put in front of you. Nothing is scheduled on the strength of a number somebody said out loud in a driveway.

What to expect

The order things happen in

  1. The roof is scoped and priced first

    An aerial measurement, an in-person color match with physical samples against your brick and trim, and a written proposal that states the products, the scope and the price. Financing is never used to work backward into a number.

  2. You ask, or you do not

    If financing is useful to you, say so and we will explain what the lending partner offers and how to reach them. If it is not, the subject is closed and nobody raises it again — not on the site visit, not in a follow-up.

  3. The application is yours, and it is with the lender

    You apply directly. The lender asks for what lenders ask for and makes its own decision. We do not see your credit file, we do not fill the application in for you, and we do not collect financial account details for anyone.

  4. You read the terms before you sign anything

    The lender's disclosures state what you will pay, over what period, at what rate, and what happens if you pay it off early. Read them at your own pace and ask us anything about the roofing side of the document. A roof that can wait a week can wait a week.

  5. The work is scheduled the same either way

    How the roof is paid for does not change the crew, the materials, the schedule, the protection of your property or the standard of the install. The same owner who wrote the proposal is the one on site when it starts.

Who decides what

Two documents, two different authors

Ours: the proposal

  • What is being done, to which part of the house, with which products and colors.
  • The measurements it is based on, and the layers underneath the visible material.
  • What we are not doing, and what we show you if we open something up and find rot.
  • The schedule, the site protection, and how the property is left each work day.
  • What we warrant about the work we performed, on the terms in that document.

Theirs: the credit agreement

  • Whether you are approved, and for how much.
  • The rate, the period, and the payment schedule.
  • Fees, prepayment terms and what happens if a payment is missed.
  • Any condition attached to the offer.

We do not restate, summarize or interpret any of that for you, and we will never present a lender's number as though it were ours. If a term is unclear, it goes to the lender in writing before you sign.

Our side of it

What we will not do

  • No pressure to sign on the day we meet, and no offer that expires because you slept on it.
  • No monthly figure quoted before the work itself has a written price.
  • No financing paperwork handed to you at the kitchen table in place of a proposal.
  • No collecting bank or card details on our side. Anything financial goes directly to the lender.
  • No claim about approval odds, rates or terms. That is the lender's to state, not ours.
  • No larger scope suggested because a payment plan makes it look affordable. The scope follows the house.

Most of what we do in OH is planned work a homeowner decided to do properly, and plenty of those decisions are made with no lender anywhere in the file. The job itself is the same either way: how a full replacement runs, and how we match what we install to the house.
