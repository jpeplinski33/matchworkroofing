Ohio Administrative Law

How Ohio Matching Rule OAC 3901-1-54 Protects You

When storm winds or hail damage a portion of your roof, insurance companies often attempt to pay for only a few damaged shingles or a single roof facet. In Ohio, you are protected by state administrative law:

"When an interior or exterior loss requires replacement of items and the replaced items do not match in quality, color, or size, the insurer shall replace as much of the item as to result in a reasonably uniform appearance." — OAC 3901-1-54(I)(1)(b)

Because asphalt shingles fade from UV exposure and manufacturers frequently discontinue older shingle molds, finding an identical replacement is nearly impossible. When we prove a non-match, your insurer is legally required to replace the entire slope or complete roof.

The 7 Items Adjusters Commonly Omit:

  • 1. Drip Edge Flashing: Mandated by Ohio Residential Building Code R905.2.8.5 along eaves and rakes.
  • 2. Ice & Water Shield: Required in Central Ohio climate zones extending 24 inches inside exterior wall lines.
  • 3. Starter Course Shingles: Required for wind resistance certification, often missing from initial adjuster scopes.
  • 4. Ridge Cap & Ventilation: Full replacement of ridge vent systems to prevent manufacturer warranty invalidation.
  • 5. Step & Counter Flashing: Chimney and wall flashing replacement required for watertight building envelope.
  • 6. Valley Metal Linings: W-valley metal or closed-cut heavy membrane specifications.
  • 7. Overhead & Profit (O&P): 10% overhead and 10% profit whenever complex multi-trade coordination is required.

⚠️ Ohio Deductible Compliance Policy

Under Ohio Revised Code Section 3999.22, it is illegal for a roofing contractor to pay, rebate, waive, or promise to pay all or part of an insurance deductible. Matchwork Roofing operates with 100% legal compliance: we work directly with your insurer’s Xactimate line items to ensure full scope coverage, and your financial obligation is strictly your legal insurance policy deductible.

Insurance Claim Questions

What happens if my insurance company denied my claim or only approved 1 slope?

You have the legal right to request a reinspection or submit a formal supplement. We provide the forensic ITEL shingle match report and Ohio OAC 3901-1-54 documentation to overturn partial denials and secure full roof approvals.

How long do I have to file a storm claim in Ohio?

Most homeowner policies in Ohio allow 12 months from the date of the storm event (date of loss) to file a claim and complete repairs. Contact us immediately to establish the meteorological storm date.
