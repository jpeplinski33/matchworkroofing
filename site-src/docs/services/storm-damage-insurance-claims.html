Repair Scope

Why Matching Matters in a Repair Scope

When wind or hail damages part of a roof, a repair estimate may cover only a few shingles or a single slope. Whether that repair is workable depends on the condition of the surrounding shingles and whether the product is still made.

Asphalt shingles weather and change color over time, and manufacturers retire products. When a matching shingle is not available, we document the product and its condition so the repair scope can be discussed with you and your insurer on the facts. How a policy handles matching depends on its terms.

Components of a Complete Roof Replacement

  • 1. Drip Edge Flashing: Metal edge at eaves and rakes that directs water off the roof deck and into the gutter.
  • 2. Ice & Water Shield: Self-adhering membrane at eaves and valleys where ice dams and wind-driven rain cause leaks.
  • 3. Starter Course Shingles: Sealed first course along eaves and rakes so the field shingles are held down at the edge.
  • 4. Ridge Cap & Ventilation: Ridge cap shingles and the intake and exhaust ventilation details appropriate to the roof.
  • 5. Step & Counter Flashing: Flashing condition and installation details where the roof meets chimneys and walls.
  • 6. Valley Metal Linings: Valley metal or membrane where two slopes meet.
  • 7. Pipe Boots & Penetrations: Flashing around plumbing vents, exhaust fans, and other roof penetrations.

Storm Damage Questions

What if my insurer approved only a partial repair?

You can ask your insurer about a re-inspection or provide additional documentation. We can provide inspection photos and shingle identification results so the repair scope can be discussed on the facts. Decisions about coverage rest with your insurer and your policy.

How soon should I document storm damage?

Promptly. Take dated photos of anything you can safely see from the ground, note the storm date, and schedule an inspection. Claim deadlines vary by policy, so check yours or ask your insurer.
