Dublin, Ohio

Dublin's HOA palettes, Hardie walls and Class 4 roofs

A Dublin house is built to a palette — brick, stone, trim and roofline chosen together. When one material fails, the replacement has to rejoin that palette, not sit beside it.

Free estimate Contact Matchwork

Housing stock

Two generations of Dublin houses, failing differently

Muirfield Village and Tartan Fields are large brick and stone colonials built from the 1980s through the 2000s, with dormers, cross-gables and porch returns on an estate-scale roofline. Toward Ballantrae and Wedgewood Hills, newer colonials carry brick fronts and simpler shapes; Historic Dublin leans to painted wood.

Brick and stone

Deep red to tan and buff brick, stone on the front elevation, trim in white or black.

Estate rooflines

More dormers and valleys, and more sightlines where a match has to hold.

What fails first

Valley metal and dormer cheeks on the older stock; side-elevation vinyl on the newer.

Four trades

Roofing, siding, gutters and masonry on a Dublin house

One crew on the house once beats three separate mobilizations against the same trim.

Roofing: Class 4 and metal accents

A Class 4 rated shingle takes a hail strike without fracturing the mat. On estate homes we also set slate-profile designer shingles and standing-seam metal on porches. Materials and scope: the roofing trade page.

Siding: Hardie on the side elevations

Fiber cement replaces the builder-grade vinyl on Dublin's side and rear walls. James Hardie holds a paint line and can be ordered inside a community's allowed color range. Soffit and fascia: the siding trade page.

Gutters: much roof, short run

Dormers concentrate water into short eave runs, where undersized gutter shows first. We form 5-inch and 6-inch seamless aluminum on site, matched to trim or brick. Profiles and drainage: the gutter trade page.

Masonry: mortar matched to the wall

Dublin brick does not want a bright new gray joint. We match mortar color, tool joints to the existing profile, tuckpoint freeze-thaw damage and rebuild chimneys. Chimney and brick work: the masonry trade page.

Color matching

Sample boards against Dublin brick, in Dublin light

A sample read under store lighting shifts a shade against tan brick in sun, so we bring physical boards to the house before ordering. On a job that must clear a review board, those boards are what you submit. Shingle to brick, siding to trim, gutter to palette: the Matchwork Method.

Before you order

Architectural review, and the paperwork that goes with it

Homes in Muirfield Village fall under the neighborhood's own architectural guidelines: exterior changes — a roof, a siding color, a gutter profile — generally clear that review before work starts. Newer subdivisions often carry HOA covenants doing the same.

We build that step into the schedule, with product names, color names and sample boards you can hand to a committee. We will not claim an approval you do not have.

Storm exposure

Hail, wind and the canopy over Dublin

Dublin sits in the same storm track as the rest of central Ohio — spring and summer systems bringing hail and derecho-grade straight-line wind. Muirfield Village's mature canopy adds limb-strike risk the newer sections see less of.

Wind-driven rain gets in at the transitions, not mid-slope, on a dormered roof — that is where we look first. Ice dams form on the steeper sections.

Repairs

When a repair is the honest answer

A lifted flashing at a dormer cheek, a popped nail line, one slope bruised by hail, a gutter hanging off rotted fascia — repair work, scoped as repair. Diagnosis and flashing: the exterior repairs page.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Realtors

Dublin listings and the review clock

Dublin's resale stock is old enough that roof, chimney and gutter remarks are routine on an inspection. Where a repair must clear an architectural review first, that step sets the timeline, so we start it before we start the work. The Close-Ready program: the realtor page.
