New Albany, Ohio

New Albany's cedar, designer shingles and estate drainage

Georgian brick and white trim leave little room for a material that is close but not right, and the lots are big enough that water has to be handled, not just caught.

Housing stock

Georgian brick, white trim and estate-scale lots

Georgian-style brick homes with white-painted trim and columned entries are New Albany's signature look, built along the community's Georgian architectural pattern. Symmetrical rooflines, decorative dormers and copper accents on porches are typical. Lots are large, setbacks deep, and cedar shake sits on a number of the older estate homes.

Georgian brick

Red or tan brick, crisp white trim, a columned entry. It shows every mismatch clearly.

Cedar and slate profiles

Cedar shake on some estate homes, slate-profile designer shingles on others.

Estate drainage

Large roof areas, long downspout runs, grading that has to carry water away from the foundation.

Four trades

Roofing, siding, gutters and masonry in New Albany

Four-trade scopes are common here: a design review looks at all four at once.

Roofing: cedar shake, designer shingles

Cedar here is specified for the look, and it needs airflow and a dry back to last in Ohio. A designer slate-profile shingle is the lower-maintenance answer, with copper accents on porches. Cedar and metal: the roofing trade page.

Siding: cedar walls, painted trim

Cedar shake siding on the older estate homes, painted wood trim, and fiber cement substitutes where the profile matters but the upkeep does not. Cedar and trim: the siding trade page.

Gutters: half-round, copper, the tie-in

An estate roof area moves a lot of water, so we hang half-round and copper on Georgian elevations, sized to the catchment. Where grading allows, downspouts tie into an underground run daylighting away from the house. Half-round and copper: the gutter trade page.

Masonry: Georgian brick and its chimneys

Brick matching matters more here than elsewhere in the metro: the trim is white and the elevation is symmetrical. We match brick and mortar, tuckpoint, and rebuild chimneys to suit the roofline. Brick matching and chimneys: the masonry trade page.

Color matching

White trim is unforgiving

White trim reads hard against brick that shifts undertone in different light, and a cedar roof weathers from orange toward silver in its first years — so the match is made against what the house will look like, not only what it looks like today. Boards come to the house. The approach: the Matchwork Method.

Before you order

Community design review, ahead of the permit

New Albany's community design standards generally call for exterior work — roof, siding and gutters included — to clear an architectural review before a permit is pulled. Material and profile are judged, not just color.

We plan the schedule around it: specification sheets, sample boards and elevation notes reach you early, and the tear-off date sits after the approval, never on an assumption about one.

Storm exposure

Open ground, mature trees, wind across big lots

New Albany sees the same seasonal pattern as the rest of central Ohio — spring and summer thunderstorms carrying hail and straight-line wind. Large lots and mature landscaping mean less shelter and more limbs over the roof planes.

Cedar shake is the material most likely to split in a hail run, and wind that lifts a shingle edge drives rain under a copper accent if the flashing is wrong.

Repairs

Cedar and copper are both repairable

Individual shakes get replaced, valleys get re-dressed, and a copper gutter or a chimney crown is usually worth fixing rather than rebuilding. Cedar, flashing and gutter repair: the exterior repairs page.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Realtors

Listings here get negotiated hard

Houses at this price point draw a close inspection, and cedar roofs, chimneys and gutter drainage are recurring items on the report. We document what we found and what we did, with photos, and work to your closing date. The Close-Ready program: the realtor page.
