Powell, Ohio

Powell's builder palettes, Hardie walls and HOA approval

Powell's subdivisions repeat the same floor plans, brick faces and siding colors street after street. The shingle is rarely the hard part; matching inside a palette that narrow, and getting it approved, is.

Housing stock

One builder palette, repeated across a lot of streets

Most of Powell, and the Liberty Township streets many Powell-address homes sit on, went up between the 1990s and the 2010s: two-story colonials with a brick front and vinyl or fiber cement on the other three sides, on simpler rooflines than the estate homes closer to Dublin. Historic Uptown Powell holds the older stock.

Brick-front colonials

Brick or stone veneer at the front, siding on the sides and rear.

Repeatable rooflines

A few builder plans repeat across many streets, so the shape is a simple gable-and-hip.

Narrow palettes

Grays, greiges and a few blues. The failure is close-but-not-quite, not a wild choice.

Four trades

Roofing, siding, gutters and masonry in Powell

Roofing: Class 4 and metal

A Class 4 rated shingle takes a Delaware County hail strike without fracturing the mat. Standing-seam metal on porch roofs reads well against a brick front. Class 4 and metal: the roofing trade page.

Siding: Hardie over builder vinyl

Vinyl on Powell's side and rear elevations warps and loosens at the nails before the brick front shows age. James Hardie fiber cement replaces it, ordered inside a covenant's color range. Hardie and trim: the siding trade page.

Gutters: long runs, pitched right

Simple gable-and-hip shapes shed fast into long eave runs, so we form seamless aluminum on site at 5-inch or 6-inch, pitched to the downspouts and matched to trim. Guards and drainage: the gutter trade page.

Masonry: brick fronts and chimneys

Powell's brick is young enough that mortar failure usually sits at the chimney or the entry veneer rather than across a wall. We match brick and mortar, tuckpoint and rebuild. Brick matching and chimneys: the masonry trade page.

Color matching

Matching inside a palette this narrow

Powell's brick and siding colors repeat across a limited set of builder palettes, so a shingle blend close to but not quite matching the house next door is an easy mistake to make from a chip alone. We compare boards against your brick and siding in daylight. The approach: the Matchwork Method.

Before you order

Getting an HOA color approval through

Many of Powell's newer subdivisions carry HOA covenants including exterior color approval, roofing included. Read yours before a color is picked: a blend that is not on the allowed list will not pass, however well it matches the house.

For the submission we hand you the practical part — manufacturer and product name, the color name as printed, a physical sample board, notes on profile and finish. The tear-off date is set behind the approval.

Storm exposure

Hail on open ground, with less to slow the wind

Powell sits at the edge of the same central Ohio storm track that crosses Dublin and Delaware County, with hail and straight-line wind. Younger trees mean less limb-strike debris, but also less wind break, so the roof takes more of the direct hit.

On a long straight eave run, a lifted shingle edge can unzip a course, and the vinyl underneath takes water.

Repairs

Repairs on builder stock

Nail pops, a lifted course on the windward slope, a gutter pulled off soft fascia, a chimney cap cracked by freeze-thaw — most 1990s and 2000s Powell stock is repairable, and a repair is often the right call. Diagnosis and flashing: the exterior repairs page.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Realtors

Powell listings and the covenant clock

Powell and Liberty Township turn over a lot of houses, and the same few remarks recur: a worn roof slope, gutter pitch, chimney flashing. Where a covenant approval is required, that paperwork is the slow part of a pre-closing repair, so it starts first. Close-Ready: the realtor page.
