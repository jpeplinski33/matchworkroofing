What It Looks Like

Slate Character Without the Slate Budget

Highland Slate's tabs are cut wide and irregular, with enough surface texture to throw the uneven shadow lines you see on a real slate roof. From the street it reads as stone, not asphalt — which is the whole appeal of the designer tier. The color palette runs to slate-appropriate tones like Black Granite, New England Slate, Smokey Quartz, Fieldstone, and Max Def Weathered Wood.

Unlike real slate, it installs like a standard asphalt shingle — no structural reinforcement, no specialty slate crew — and unlike the multi-layer luxury lines, it gets to the slate look at a friendlier material cost.

Who It Suits

A Step Up From Architectural

Highland Slate fits Central Ohio homeowners who want their roof to be a design feature rather than background — think the brick Colonials of Upper Arlington, the stone-accented builds in Dublin, or a New Albany custom home where a flat shingle would underdress the elevation. It suits steep, visible rooflines especially well, since that's where the texture shows.

It carries a Class 3 impact rating — the same as the Landmark architectural line. If your neighborhood sees regular hail and you want the slate look with the highest impact classification, compare it with Belmont or Carriage House, both Class 4, below.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between Class 3 and Class 4 impact ratings?

Both come from UL 2218, the industry impact test that drops steel balls onto the shingle from set heights. Class 4 is the highest classification — a larger impact, survived without cracking — and Class 3 is one step below it. Highland Slate is Class 3; if you want the slate look in Class 4, look at Belmont or Carriage House. Some insurers discount premiums for Class 4 roofs, so ask your agent what your policy offers.

How is Highland Slate different from Belmont?

Both mimic natural slate. Highland Slate is a broad single-layer shingle — the more attainable way into the slate look, with a Class 3 impact rating. Belmont is the step up: a Class 4 impact rating and deeper dimensionality, aimed at steep-slope luxury roofs. We price both on your estimate so you can weigh the difference against your budget.

Who handles the manufacturer coverage on my shingles?

Manufacturer warranties are provided and administered by CertainTeed — ask us which manufacturer's warranty applies to the shingles on your estimate, and review the full terms on CertainTeed's warranty page.
