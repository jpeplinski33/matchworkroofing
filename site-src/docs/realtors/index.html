Who this is for

Both sides of the table, and the managers in between

Listing agents

A house that photographs well and survives an inspection. We do the pre-listing work — the roof detail, the rotted trim, the gutter run, the chimney crown — before the sign goes in the yard, not during a remedy negotiation.

Buyer agents

You have a report, a remedy window and a buyer who wants the house. Send us the report. We separate what has to be fixed from what does not, and complete the work with documentation the buyer and the lender both accept.

Property managers

Rental and small multifamily portfolios: one contractor across roofing, siding, gutters and masonry, so a turn is not four vendors and four invoices. We work to your vacancy window and document it for the owner's file.

The report

We read it line by line before anybody spends money

A home inspection report is written for a buyer, not for a contractor. It lists observations in the same voice — a loose ridge cap and a sagging deck read about the same on the page. Our job is to sort the list into what has to be repaired, in what order, by which trade.

Legitimate

A real failure with a real water path or a real structural consequence. We scope it and repair it.

Monitor

Wear normal for the age of the house that will not affect this transaction. We say so in writing, and say what would change the answer.

Not ours

Electrical, plumbing, HVAC, radon, foundation, appliances. Not a roofing, siding, gutter or masonry item, and we say that rather than quote it.

We translate the inspector's findings into a repair scope. We do not override the inspector and will never tell a buyer an observation is wrong because it is inconvenient. If we disagree with an item, you get our reasoning and the photographs, and the finding still stands in the file.

Two honest paths

Repair now and close, or run the claim properly

We will tell you which one the house is on. Sometimes it is both, on different parts of the same roof.

Path one: repair now and close

Wear, bad original details, a leaking pipe boot, rotted fascia, open gutter seams, failing mortar, a chimney crown that has cracked. Ordinary scoped repairs, done to your closing date, documented for the buyer's file. This is most of what we do for agents.

Path two: this is storm damage

If what we find is hail or wind damage, the honest route is a claim, run properly, with dated photographs and a scope an adjuster can read. We do not suggest a repair is a way around a claim, and we do not tell anybody their claim will be approved. You choose your contractor. The process is on storm damage and insurance claims.

The repair menu

Four trades on one scope, matching included

One crew for the whole exterior: the roof repair, the siding patch, the gutter run and the chimney flashing get scheduled together instead of around each other.

Roofing

Leaks, flashing, pipe boots, skylight curbs, ridge caps, wind-lifted tabs, ice-dam damage, soft deck. Matching the existing shingle is part of the scope, not an upgrade. See roofing repairs in Columbus.

Siding

Cracked panels, corner posts, trim, soffit and fascia rot, kickout flashing at roof-to-wall transitions. Discontinued siding is the common remedy problem here: discontinued siding matching. See also siding repairs.

Gutters

Open seams, wrong pitch, fasteners pulling out of rotted fascia, downspouts dumping at the foundation. Colour matched to trim, brick or roof line so a new run does not read as new in listing photos. See gutter repairs and downspout drainage.

Masonry

Tuckpointing, spalled brick, chimney crowns, caps, flashing and rebuilds, stucco at roof transitions. Brick matching is the hard part, because the brick on a Columbus house may no longer be made: brick matching and repair, masonry repairs.

The transaction file

What the buyer, the lender and the next inspector get to see

Repairs on a transaction are half work and half paper. You get both in one file you can forward.

  • A dated roof inspection report: observed condition, photographs, what we repaired, what we did not touch
  • Dated before, during and after photographs, shot from the same angle so the change is obvious
  • A written scope, signed off before work starts, listing materials and the colour matched
  • A completion letter for the buyer's file: what was done, where, with what material, on what date
  • The workmanship warranty, stated as repair-only, in the same words every time

A roof inspection report from us is a dated statement of what we observed and what we did. It is not a promise about the years ahead and not a warranty on the roof system. The document says so on its face.

What we warrant

Matchwork warrants the repairs we performed, on the terms in your proposal. A repair is not a warranty on the rest of the roof, the siding, the gutters or the masonry we did not touch, and we will never present it as one.

Two different jobs

Pre-listing updates and in-contract remediation

Pre-listing

There is room to do it right. We sequence the work around photographers, stagers and first showings, and we take the time to match properly, because a patch that reads as a patch shows up in listing photos and again in the inspection. We will also tell you which items a buyer's inspector will find anyway.

In contract

The remedy window is fixed and the closing date is fixed, so the scope has to fit inside them. We work to your closing date: the site visit is scheduled around showings and occupancy, the scope covers what the report actually requires, and the file is built as the work happens. If something cannot honestly be repaired inside that window, you hear it early enough to renegotiate.

How agents work with us

From the report in your inbox to the file in the buyer's hands

  1. Send the report

    The whole PDF is more useful than a screenshot of one line. Seller or inspector photographs help too. Upload them below or email the desk address.

    We work to your closing date. You get the completion letter, the dated photographs and the repair-only warranty in one file. Payment is due at completion.

Boundaries

What we will not do

  • Warrant a roof we did not replace. Our warranty covers the repairs we performed and nothing we did not touch, on the page and in the document.
  • Put our name on a paper that says a roof is sound for years to come. We write down what we observed on a dated inspection, what we repaired, and what we excluded. Anything stronger would be a promise about weather we do not control.
  • Put pricing on this page. Roof area, pitch, deck condition, material availability and how much of a wall has to come apart all change the number. You get a written scope for the house in front of us.
  • Show up on a seller's lawn as a storm-chaser. No door-knocking after a weather event, no canvassing, no ladder thrown over a hedge. We come when an agent or an owner asks us to.
  • Tell you a claim will be approved, or that a partial scope will become a full one. Nobody outside your carrier can promise that.
