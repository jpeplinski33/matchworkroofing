Hail Impact vs. Thermal Blistering: How Ohio Adjusters Evaluate Damage

When an insurance adjuster inspects your roof after a Central Ohio hail storm, their most common denial reason is: "That's not storm damage, that's manufacturer blistering." Here is the forensic difference and how to prove real impact fractures.

The Critical Legal Distinction:

Hail impact is an external sudden storm loss covered under standard HO-3 homeowner policies. Blistering is caused by trapped moisture gas pockets inside the asphalt during manufacturing or severe attic heat. Adjusters often confuse the two—unless you provide forensic evidence.

1. Forensic Comparison: Hail Bruise vs. Thermal Blister

⛈️ Genuine Hail Damage

  • • Soft / Spongy Bruise: Pressing on the circular indent feels soft like an apple bruise due to fractured fiberglass substrate beneath.
  • • Directional Pattern: Impacts correlate with wind direction and soft metal damage (dents on ridge caps, gutters, AC fins).
  • • Granule Embedment: Granules are driven deep into the asphalt mat by the kinetic strike.

🔥 Manufacturing / Heat Blisters

  • • Hard Rim Surface: Blisters form hollow, raised domes with crisp edges like popped bubbles.
  • • Random Orientation: Occurs uniformly across non-impact slopes without corresponding soft metal dents.
  • • No Substrate Fracture: The underlying woven fiberglass matting remains rigid and unbroken.

2. The Ohio Matching Law Superpower (OAC 3901-1-54)

If your roof sustains legitimate hail fractures, Ohio insurance law protects you against mismatched patch repairs:

"When replacement items do not match in quality, color, or size, the insurer shall replace as much of the item as to result in a reasonably uniform appearance." — OAC 3901-1-54

If your damaged shingle is discontinued (e.g. older Owens Corning Oakridge or CertainTeed Horizon lines), the carrier cannot force a single-slope patch with a mismatched shingle. They must replace the entire roof to maintain architectural uniformity.
