Hail Impact vs. Blistering: How to Tell Them Apart

After a Central Ohio hail storm, one question comes up often: is that mark hail damage or blistering? You may hear it described as "That's not storm damage, that's manufacturer blistering." Here is how the two differ and why it matters.

The Short Version:

Hail impact is sudden damage from a storm. Blistering comes from gas pockets in the asphalt during manufacturing or from heat buildup over time. They can look similar from a distance, so a close inspection and clear photos matter.

1. Hail Bruise vs. Heat Blister

Hail Impact

  • • Soft / Spongy Bruise: Some impacts bruise or fracture the shingle mat. A visible mark alone does not establish the cause or extent of damage.
  • • Directional Pattern: Hits tend to follow the storm direction, and soft metals like gutters, vents, and ridge caps often show dents too.
  • • Granule Displacement: Granules are knocked loose or pressed into the asphalt at the point of impact.

Blistering

  • • Hard Rim Surface: Blisters are raised or popped bubbles in the asphalt with a defined rim.
  • • Random Pattern: Blistering may have a scattered pattern. Metal dents and other surrounding conditions can help an inspector evaluate the cause.
  • • Underlying Condition: The condition of the mat needs to be assessed; surface appearance alone may be inconclusive.

2. Why Matching Matters for Repairs

If part of the roof is damaged, the next question is whether a repair will match the rest of the roof.

Shingles weather over time and many older products are no longer made, so a spot repair may not match in color or size. Documenting the product and its condition helps define a repair scope that looks right. How your policy treats matching depends on its terms, so ask your insurer.
