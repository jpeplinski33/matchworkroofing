Nail Pops Explained: Why Roofing Nails Back Out & Cause Hidden Leaks

A "nail pop" is one of the most common yet deceptive roof defects in Central Ohio. A single backing-out fastener can push right through a shingle, creating an invisible water funnel directly into your attic.

What Is a Nail Pop?

A nail pop occurs when a galvanized roofing nail loosens from the plywood decking and pushes upward against the underside of the overlapping shingle, eventually piercing the asphalt layer and allowing rainwater to trickle down the nail shank into the roof deck.

1. The 3 Primary Causes of Nail Pops in Central Ohio

  • Thermal Expansion & Contraction: Ohio's extreme temperature swings (from 90°F summer humidity to sub-zero winter freezes) cause OSB and plywood decking to expand and contract, gradually wiggling smooth-shank nails free.
  • Improper Pneumatic Nail Gun Pressure: When unskilled installers set air compressor PSI incorrectly, nails are either "overdriven" (cutting the shingle mat) or "underdriven" (left crooked and sticking up).
  • Poor Attic Ventilation: Excess attic moisture in winter softens the underside of the roof decking, weakening the wood grain's grip around the nail shank.

📸 Field Inspection: Golf Village, Powell OH

During a routine inspection on a 12-year-old Powell home, our inspectors identified 14 active nail pops on the west slope.

Diagnostic Finding: 4 nail heads had fully pierced through the top shingle laminate, causing slow wood rot on the rafter below.
Prevention Standard: Matchwork installs CertainTeed NailTrak® wide-fastening systems with ring-shank nails to eliminate fastener back-out permanently.
